title: Implementing the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026
---
 
By the authority vested in me as President by the Constitution and the
laws of the United States of America, including the Lindsey O. Graham
Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 (22 U.S.C. 10802 *et seq**.*)
(Graham Act), and section 301 of title 3, United States Code, I hereby
determine and order:

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Section</span>
<span style="text-decoration: underline">1</span>. 
<span style="text-decoration: underline">Background</span>.  On
September 18, 2026, I signed the Graham Act into law.  The Graham Act
provides for the imposition of certain sanctions with respect to foreign
persons, restrictions with respect to persons, and duties with respect
to goods imported into the United States from the Russian Federation and
certain other countries.

Section 115 of the Graham Act authorizes the President to waive the
application of any sanctions provision with respect to a foreign person,
any restriction with respect to a person, and any duty under the Graham
Act, subject to the submission to the Congress of a written
certification that such a waiver is in the national interests of the
United States and a report explaining the basis for the certification. 

As described further in section 4 of this order, I am hereby waiving the
application of such sanctions, restrictions, and duties provided for in
the Graham Act.  I have submitted to the Congress a certification that
the issuance of such a waiver at this time is in the national interests
of the United States.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">2</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">Increases in Rate of
Duty</span>.  (a)  The rate of duty on goods from the Russian Federation
shall be increased by 500 percent *ad valorem*, subject to the
exceptions set forth in section 114 of the Graham Act and the waivers
issued in section 4 of this order.

(b)  The rate of duty on goods from the largest 5 importers of crude oil
and natural gas that originated in the Russian Federation shall be
increased by 100 percent *ad valorem* if the country knowingly makes new
purchases of crude oil or natural gas that originated in the Russian
Federation on or after October 18, 2026, subject to the exceptions set
forth in section 114 of the Graham Act and the waivers issued in section
4 of this order.  The United States Trade Representative (Trade
Representative), in consultation with the Secretary of State, the
Secretary of the Treasury, the Secretary of Energy, and any other senior
executive branch official that the Trade Representative determines
appropriate, shall monitor the circumstances related to the importation
and purchases by countries of crude oil and natural gas of Russian
Federation origin, regularly inform the President of any such activity,
and provide recommendations for further action, as provided for in
section 3 of this order.   

(c)  The rate of duty on goods from the top 5 facilitators of Russian
oil sanctions evasion shall be increased by 100 percent *ad valorem*,
subject to the exceptions set forth in section 114 of the Graham Act and
the waivers issued in section 4 of this order.  The Trade
Representative, in consultation with the Secretary of State, the
Secretary of the Treasury, the Secretary of Energy, and any other senior
executive branch official that the Trade Representative determines
appropriate, shall monitor the activities of countries that facilitate
Russian oil sanctions evasion, as defined in section 113(i) of the
Graham Act, and report to the President on which countries are among the
top 5 such facilitators.

(d)  The increases in the rates of duty provided by this order shall
apply in addition to any other duty, fee, tax, exaction, or charge
applicable with respect to the good, including any duty imposed under
title VII of the Tariff Act of 1930 (19 U.S.C. 1671 *et seq*.), sections
122, 201, or 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. 2132, 2251, and
2411), or section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962 (19 U.S.C.
1862).

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">3</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">Monitoring</span>.  The Trade
Representative, in consultation with the Secretary of State, the
Secretary of the Treasury, the Secretary of Energy, and any other senior
executive branch official that the Trade Representative determines
appropriate, shall monitor the circumstances related to the importation
and purchases by countries of crude oil and natural gas of Russian
Federation origin, as well as the facilitation of Russian sanctions
evasion by countries, and regularly report to the President on those
circumstances.  The Trade Representative, in consultation with the
Secretary of State, the Secretary of the Treasury, the Secretary of
Energy, and any other senior executive branch official that the Trade
Representative determines appropriate, shall promptly inform the
President of any circumstance that, in his opinion, might indicate the
need for further action by the President, including with respect to the
waivers.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">4</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">Waive</span>r.  (a)  Pursuant
to section 115(a) of the Graham Act, I hereby waive the application of
any duties provided for in sections 112 and 113 of the Graham Act or
this order.  I have submitted to the Congress a certification that the
issuance of such a waiver is in the national interests of the United
States and a report explaining the basis for the certification,
consistent with section 115(b) of the Graham Act.

     (b)  Pursuant to section 115(a) of the Graham Act, I hereby waive
the application of the sanctions provision provided for in
section 102(d) of the Graham Act with respect to foreign persons and the
restrictions provided for in section 102(d) of the Graham Act with
respect to persons.  For the avoidance of doubt, nothing in this order
affects any sanctions or other measures provided for under any Executive
Order issued to address the national emergency that I continued on March
24, 2026, with respect to specified harmful foreign activities of the
Government of the Russian Federation (91 *Fed*. *Reg*. 15515), as in
effect on the day before the date of enactment of the Graham Act,
including with respect to all persons sanctioned under any such
Executive Order.  I have submitted to the Congress a certification that
the issuance of such a waiver is in the national interests of the United
States and a report explaining the basis for the certification,
consistent with section 115(b) of the Graham Act.

(c)  Pursuant to section 115(a) of the Graham Act, I hereby waive the
application of the following sanctions provisions with respect to
foreign persons and restrictions with respect to persons:  sections
102(a), 102(e), 103, 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109, 110, 111, and 117 of
the Graham Act.  I have submitted to the Congress a certification that
the issuance of such a waiver is in the national interests of the United
States and a report explaining the basis for the certification,
consistent with section 115(b) of the Graham Act.

(d)  The waivers described in subsections (a) through (c) of this
section shall remain in effect until such time as the President or the
Secretary of State, in consultation with the Secretary of the Treasury
and the Trade Representative, as appropriate, determine that further
action is appropriate. 

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">5</span>. 
<span style="text-decoration: underline">Delegation of
Authorities</span>.  (a)  I hereby delegate the functions and
authorities vested in the President by the Graham Act as follows:

\(i\)     sections 102(a), 102(b), 102(c), 104(a), 108(b), 111(b), and
114(a)(2), to the Secretary of State, in consultation with the Secretary
of the Treasury, and to the Secretary of the Treasury, in consultation
with the Secretary of State;

\(ii\)    section 115 to the Secretary of State, in consultation with
the Secretary of the Treasury and the Trade Representative, as
appropriate;

(iii)   sections 102(e)(1), 103(a), 103(b), 103(d), 104(b), 110(a),
110(c), and 116(a) to the Secretary of the Treasury, in consultation
with the Secretary of State;

\(iv\)    section 111(a) to the Secretary of Energy, in consultation
with the Secretary of State, the Secretary of Commerce, and the
Secretary of the Treasury;

(v)     section 112 to the Trade Representative, in consultation with
the Secretary of State and the Secretary of the Treasury;

\(vi\)    section 113(a) to the Trade Representative, in consultation
with the Secretary of State, the Secretary of the Treasury, and the
Secretary of Energy;

(vii)   section 117(a), with respect to the termination of the
application of any sanction with respect to a foreign person or any
restriction with respect to a person, to the Secretary of State, in
consultation with the Secretary of the Treasury; and to the Secretary of
the Treasury, in consultation with the Secretary of State; and

(viii)  section 117(a), with respect to the termination of any duty, to
the Trade Representative, in consultation with the Secretary of State,
the Secretary of Energy, and the Secretary of the Treasury.

(b)  The delegations in this section shall apply to any provisions of
any future public laws that are the same or substantially the same as
the provisions referenced in this section. 

(c)  The Secretary of State, the Secretary of the Treasury, the Trade
Representative, and the Secretary of Energy may, consistent with
applicable law, redelegate within their respective departments any of
the functions or authorities delegated to them by this order.  All
executive departments and agencies shall take all appropriate measures
within their authority to carry out the provisions of this order.

(d)  Any reference in this section to the Graham Act shall be deemed to
be a reference to the Graham Act as amended from time to time.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">6</span>. 
<span style="text-decoration: underline">Severability</span>.  If any
provision of this order or the application of any provision of this
order to any individual or circumstance is held to be invalid, the
remainder of this order and the application of its provisions to any
other individuals or circumstances shall not be affected.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">7</span>. 
<span style="text-decoration: underline">General Provisions</span>.  (a)
 Nothing in this order shall be construed to impair or otherwise affect:

\(i\)   the authority granted by law to an executive department or
agency, or the head thereof; or

\(ii\)  the functions of the Director of the Office of Management and
Budget relating to budgetary, administrative, or legislative proposals.

\(b\)  This order shall be implemented consistent with applicable law
and subject to the availability of appropriations.

\(c\)  This order is not intended to, and does not, create any right or
benefit, substantive or procedural, enforceable at law or in equity by
any party against the United States, its departments, agencies, or
entities, its officers, employees, or agents, or any other person.

(d)  The costs for publication of this order shall be borne by the
Department of State.

                               DONALD J. TRUMP

THE WHITE HOUSE,
