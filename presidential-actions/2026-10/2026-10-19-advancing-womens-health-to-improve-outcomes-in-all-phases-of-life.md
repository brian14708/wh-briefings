Executive Order 14437

By the authority vested in me as President by the Constitution and the
laws of the United States of America, it is hereby ordered:

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Section</span>
<span style="text-decoration: underline">1</span>. 
<span style="text-decoration: underline">Purpose and Policy</span>.  My
Administration is committed to advancing women’s health and ensuring
that medical research, diagnosis, prevention, and treatment reflect the
highest quality scientific evidence, with the goal of improving health
outcomes in all phases of life.

Despite considerable advances in biomedical science, many diseases and
conditions that disproportionately or uniquely affect women have not
received the level of research, clinical attention, and treatment that
other diseases and conditions have received.  More research is needed in
critical aspects of women’s health, including cardiovascular and heart
disease, cancer, stroke, Alzheimer’s Disease, autoimmune and
inflammatory conditions, osteoporosis, mental health, chronic pain,
endometriosis, fibroids, polyendocrine metabolic ovary syndrome,
infertility, maternal health, menopause, neurological disorders, aging,
and more.  Improving health outcomes for women requires coordinated
Federal leadership, bolstered scientific investment, and accelerated
translation of research into clinical and community practice. 

While previous administrations have invested in women’s health research,
there is a need to enhance, streamline, and elevate Federal efforts
regarding both research and the critical step of translation into health
solutions.  My Administration is committed to ensuring that American
women are receiving the best scientifically-supported medical advice in
the world.  Therefore, it is the policy of the United States to
prioritize translating research into improved clinical care that
benefits women and girls through a comprehensive approach to women’s
health that recognizes evolving health needs from infancy, childhood,
and adolescence through pregnancy, midlife, menopause, and aging, and
supports timely, stage-specific prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and
care.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">2</span>. 
<span style="text-decoration: underline">Establishment of the Women’s
Health Division Within the National Institutes of Health</span>.  (a) 
The Secretary of Health and Human Services, acting through the Director
of the National Institutes of Health (NIH), shall establish a Women’s
Health Division within an appropriate institute or center within 90
days.

(b)  The Women’s Health Division shall, in coordination with the NIH
Office of Research on Women’s Health:

(i)     increase the scientific review of women’s health conditions in
all phases of life that remain understudied or insufficiently
understood;

\(ii\)    advance and coordinate research on women’s health conditions
at all phases of life, including replication studies;

\(iii\)   promote innovation and advanced research technologies to
accelerate time to diagnosis and improve access to high-quality
prevention, treatment, specialized healthcare services, personalized
medicine, and coordinated care delivery for women’s health conditions;

\(iv\)    strengthen interdisciplinary collaboration to advance basic
and clinical research on women’s health;

(v)     support data-collection methodologies and research methodologies
that improve understanding of sex-specific health outcomes and increase
inclusion of women and girls in clinical trials;

\(vi\)    develop accountability for grant recipients to enhance
adherence to the NIH policy requiring research to account for sex as a
biological variable; and

\(vii\)   facilitate translation of scientific discoveries into clinical
practice and public health implementation across Federal, State, and
local jurisdictions.

\(c\)  The Women’s Health Division shall work with all relevant Federal
partners to continue to expand strategic coordination and policy
leadership on, career development in, and advancement of women’s health
research across disciplines.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">3</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">Establishing the White House
Women’s Health Task Force</span>.  (a)  There is hereby established the
White House Women’s Health Task Force (Task Force) co-chaired by the
Secretary of Health and Human Services and the Assistant to the
President and Chief of Staff.

\(b\)  In addition to the Co-Chairs, the Task Force shall include the
following officials:

\(i\)     the Secretary of War;

\(ii\)    the Secretary of Agriculture;

\(iii\)   the Secretary of Labor;

(iv)    the Secretary of Education;

(v)     the Secretary of Veterans Affairs;

\(vi\)    the Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency;

\(vii\)   the Commissioner of Food and Drugs;

\(viii\)  the Director of the Centers for Disease Control and
Prevention;

(ix)    the Director of the National Institutes of Health;

\(x\)     the Director of the Advanced Research Projects Agency for
Health;

(xi)    the Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid
Services; and

\(xii\)   other members of my Administration invited to participate, at
the discretion of the Co-Chairs.

\(c\)  The Co-Chairs may hold public hearings, meetings, roundtables,
and similar events, as appropriate, and may receive expert input from
leaders in women’s health, technology, academia, the private sector,
philanthropy, and other relevant subject matter areas to inform
coordinated action with measurable results.

(d)  The Department of Health and Human Services shall provide funding
for the administrative costs of the Task Force.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">4</span>. 
<span style="text-decoration: underline">Task Force Functions</span>. 
(a)  The Task Force shall ensure that the Federal Government pursues a
comprehensive approach to women’s health that recognizes evolving health
needs from infancy, childhood, and adolescence through pregnancy,
midlife, menopause, and aging, and supports necessary policy
and regulatory reforms to ensure timely and stage-specific prevention,
diagnosis, treatment, and care for women.  To achieve those goals, the
Task Force shall focus on:

\(i\)    advancing the science of women’s health through strengthening
research and initiatives across the Federal Government to improve
prevention, early detection, diagnosis, treatment, and long-term health
outcomes for women and girls, including through acceleration of the
development of new therapies and cures;

(ii)   accelerating proven, measurable, and evidence-based strategies to
increase innovation, translation, adoption, and implementation of
women’s health research into policies, evidence-based medicine, and best
clinical practices, including through updated clinical education based
on the best available science.  These efforts shall prioritize
grantmaking focused on prevention, treatment, and long-term health by
promoting early identification and management of health risks throughout
infancy, childhood, adolescence, reproductive years, midlife, menopause,
and aging, to reduce the burden of chronic disease and improve long-term
health outcomes;

\(iii\)  advancing patient-centered care by supporting education and
research into coordinated, preventive, and woman-centered care as well
as integration and implementation across primary care, specialty care,
and behavioral health providers, including through ensuring a robust
supply chain of providers and medical products to deliver high-quality
clinical care.  These efforts may include advising heads of executive
departments and agencies (agency heads) on opportunities to implement
pilot programs that improve incentives to integrate prevention, early
intervention, and treatment and remove obsolete silos between agencies,
programs, or systems, in each case as deemed appropriate by the agency
head and consistent with applicable law; and 

\(iv\)   consulting with States, tribal nations, local jurisdictions,
community-based organizations, faith-based organizations, the private
sector, philanthropic entities, and other relevant stakeholders on the
best strategies to translate research into practice.  This consultation
will ensure that scientific and technological advances in women’s health
research are effectively incorporated into evidence‑based policies and
integrated across clinical practice, public health programs, and
community-level initiatives.  Such collaboration shall accelerate
innovation, strengthen public-private partnerships, promote investment
in women’s health solutions, and support the broad adoption of
evidence-based interventions by both healthcare providers and women to
improve women’s health outcomes.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">5</span>. 
<span style="text-decoration: underline">Revocation of Prior
Actions</span>.  The following executive actions are hereby revoked: 

(a)  Executive Order 14120 of March 18, 2024 (Advancing Women’s Health
Research and Innovation).

\(b\)  Presidential Memorandum of November 13, 2023 (White House
Initiative on Women’s Health Research).

Sec. 6.  General Provisions.  (a)  Nothing in this order shall be
construed to impair or otherwise affect:

\(i\)   the authority granted by law to an executive department or
agency, or the head thereof; or

(ii)  the functions of the Director of the Office of Management and
Budget relating to budgetary, administrative, or legislative proposals.

(b)  This order shall be implemented consistent with applicable law and
subject to the availability of appropriations.

(c)  This order is not intended to, and does not, create any right or
benefit, substantive or procedural, enforceable at law or in equity by
any party against the United States, its departments, agencies, or
entities, its officers, employees, or agents, or any other person.

(d)  The costs for publication of this order shall be borne by the
Department of Health and Human Services.

                             DONALD J. TRUMP

THE WHITE HOUSE,

October 19, 2026.
