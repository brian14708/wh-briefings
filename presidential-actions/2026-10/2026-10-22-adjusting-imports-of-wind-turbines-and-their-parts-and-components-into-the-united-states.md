BY THE PRESIDENT OF THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA

A PROCLAMATION

1.  Within the past 90 days, the Secretary of Commerce (Secretary)
transmitted to me a report on his investigation into the effects of
imports of wind turbines and their parts and components on the national
security of the United States under section 232 of the Trade Expansion
Act of 1962, as amended, 19 U.S.C. 1862 (section 232).  Based on the
facts considered in that investigation, and taking into account the
close relation of the economic welfare of the Nation to our national
security and other relevant factors, *see* section 232(d) (19 U.S.C.
1862(d)), the Secretary found and advised me of his opinion that wind
turbines and their components and subcomponents are being imported into
the United States under such circumstances as to threaten to impair the
national security of the United States and provided me recommendations
for action under section 232 to adjust the imports of wind turbines and
their components and subcomponents so that such imports will not
threaten to impair the national security of the United States.

2.  Among other things, the Secretary found that wind energy is an
unreliable and intermittent energy source and that the use of wind
energy diverts the United States electrical grid from more reliable
energy sources.  The Secretary also found that products used to generate
and use wind energy pose serious national security concerns, including
by relying on foreign parts that contain cybersecurity vulnerabilities
that could lead to remote control access to wind turbines and cause
power disruption; undermining the United States’ electrical grid, and
potentially causing disruptions to critical infrastructure; interfering
with national airspace and air safety; and causing radar interference
that can affect air traffic control, weather forecasting, homeland
security, and national defense missions.  And the Secretary found that
imports of wind turbine components and subcomponents have increased over
the last decade, and that United States wind energy is reliant on
imports of foreign produced wind turbine components and subcomponents.

3.  The Secretary found that wind energy is intermittent and burdensome
on the United States electrical grid.  Wind energy is inherently
intermittent, and this unpredictability undermines wind’s suitability as
a standalone energy source compared to the steady output of fossil fuel
or nuclear energy, particularly if used for critical infrastructure. 
And even though wind energy comprises only a small portion of total
utility scale power generated in the United States, grid operators must
frequently rely upon fast-ramping fossil fuel backup generators to
compensate for wind energy’s high net load variability and maintain grid
stability.  This results in higher operational costs and increased
maintenance burdens.

4.  The Secretary found that the unpredictability of wind energy makes
it less reliable than other energy sources that conversely provide
steady output as standalone energy sources.  Given wind energy’s
intermittent reliability, use of wind energy diverts the United States
electrical grid from more reliable energy sources.  And resources that
are used to support wind energy and wind energy projects could instead
have been used to strengthen more reliable energy sources that are
essential to the national and economic security of the United States.

5.  The Secretary found that the use of wind energy poses unique
national security risks compared to other energy sources. Among other
things, wind energy products have serious cybersecurity vulnerabilities
that can cause power disruption; interfere with radar, thus
significantly affecting air traffic control, weather forecasting,
homeland security, and national defense missions; and burden the United
States electrical grid, including by diverting the United States
electrical grid from more reliable energy sources.

6.  More specifically, the Secretary found that wind energy products —
particularly foreign imported products — present unique cybersecurity
risks that could lead to remote control access to wind turbines and
cause power disruption.  Inverters are a crucial piece of electrical
equipment for using wind energy, as inverters convert the electrical
energy generated by wind turbines into a form of energy that can be used
by the power grid or local systems.  The vast majority of these
inverters are produced outside the United States and imported into the
United States.  But modern networked inverters used in wind farms —
particularly those produced and imported from outside the United States
— are an exploitable vulnerability that can allow remote access and
interference with United States power systems.  Attackers could gain
remote control over power generation, which presents risks of power
disruption or sabotage of installation facilities.

7.  The Secretary found that wind turbines can affect ground and
air-based radars by creating “clutter,” reducing detection sensitivity,
obscuring potential targets, and scattering radio wave returns.  For
example, clutter from wind projects obscures legitimate moving targets
and generates false targets in the vicinity of the wind projects, and
some mitigation techniques can lead to missing actual targets.  These
circumstances thus pose grave national security risks to the United
States, particularly in response to threats (e.g., unauthorized
aircraft) targeting sensitive United States critical infrastructure. 
And degraded radar performance from wind turbines can also have negative
implications on weather forecasting, air traffic management, and
military radar systems. 

8.  The Secretary found that the United States is reliant on imports of
foreign produced wind turbine components and subcomponents and that
imports of wind turbine components and subcomponents have increased over
the last decade, while domestic manufacturing capacity for certain
products, such as blades, has not meaningfully increased. 

9.  In light of these findings and the other findings in the Secretary’s
report, the Secretary recommended a range of actions to adjust the
imports of wind turbines and their components and subcomponents so that
such imports will not threaten to impair the national security.

10.  Moreover, in Proclamation 9704 of March 8, 2018 (Adjusting Imports
of Aluminum Into the United States), Proclamation 9705 of March 8, 2018
(Adjusting Imports of Steel Into the United States), and Proclamation
10962 of July 30, 2025 (Adjusting Imports of Copper Into the United
States), I found that aluminum, steel, and copper are being imported
into the United States in such quantities and under such circumstances
as to threaten to impair the national security of the United States and
took action under section 232 to address the national security threats
found in those proclamations.  Certain wind turbine components and
subcomponents are made of significant amounts of aluminum, steel, or
copper and contribute to the national security threat found in
Proclamation 9704, Proclamation 9705, and Proclamation 10962. 
Accordingly, certain wind turbine components and subcomponents are
subject to the tariffs imposed in Proclamation 9704, as amended;
Proclamation 9705, as amended; or Proclamation 10962, as amended, to
address the national security threats found in those proclamations.

11.  After considering the Secretary’s report, the factors in section
232(d) (19 U.S.C. 1862(d)), and other relevant factors and information,
I concur with the Secretary’s finding that wind turbines and their
components and subcomponents are being imported into the United States
under such circumstances as to threaten to impair the national security
of the United States.

12.  In my judgment, and in light of the Secretary’s report, the factors
in section 232(d) (19 U.S.C. 1862(d)), and other relevant factors and
information, I determine that it is necessary and appropriate to enter
into negotiations with foreign trading partners to adjust the imports of
wind turbines and their components and subcomponents to address the
national security threat found in this proclamation. 

13.  I therefore direct the Secretary and the United States Trade
Representative (Trade Representative) to jointly pursue negotiation of
agreements or continue any current negotiations of agreements to address
the threatened impairment of the national security with respect to
imported wind turbines and their components and subcomponents, with any
foreign trading partner the Secretary and the Trade Representative deem
appropriate, and to update me on the progress of such negotiations
within 180 days of the date of this proclamation.  Under current
circumstances, this plan of action is necessary and appropriate to
address the threatened impairment of the national security found in this
proclamation.  Depending on the status or outcome of such negotiations,
I may consider alternative remedies in the future. 

14.  Section 232 authorizes the President to take action to adjust the
imports of an article and its derivatives that are being imported into
the United States in such quantities or under such circumstances as to
threaten to impair the national security of the United States.  Section
232 includes the authority to adopt and carry out a plan of action, with
adjustments over time, to address the national security threat.  That
initial plan of action may include negotiations of agreements along with
other actions to adjust imports to address the national security threat,
including tariffs.  Moreover, if action under section 232 includes the
negotiation of an agreement, such as one contemplated in section
232(c)(3)(A)(i) (19 U.S.C. 1862(c)(3)(A)(i)), the President may also
take other actions he deems necessary to adjust imports so that such
imports will not threaten to impair the national security of the United
States, including if such an agreement is not entered into within 180
days of the date of this proclamation or is not being carried out or is
ineffective.  *See* 19 U.S.C. 1862(c)(3)(A).

15.  Section 604 of the Trade Act of 1974, as amended (19 U.S.C. 2483)
(section 604), authorizes the President to embody in the Harmonized
Tariff Schedule of the United States the substance of statutes affecting
import treatment, and actions thereunder, including the removal,
modification, continuance, or imposition of any rate of duty or other
import restriction.

NOW, THEREFORE, I, DONALD J. TRUMP, President of the United States of
America, by the authority vested in me by the Constitution and the laws
of the United States, including section 232; section 604; and section
301 of title 3, United States Code, do hereby proclaim as follows:

(1)  The Secretary and the Trade Representative, and any senior
executive branch official they deem appropriate, shall jointly pursue or
continue pursuing negotiations of agreements to address the national
security threat found in this proclamation.

(2)  The Secretary and the Trade Representative, in consultation with
any other senior executive branch officials they deem appropriate,
shall, from time to time, update me on the status or outcome of the
negotiations described in this proclamation.  The Secretary and the
Trade Representative shall provide at least one of these updates within
180 days of the date of this proclamation.

(3)  The Secretary shall continue to monitor imports of wind turbines
and their components and subcomponents.  The Secretary also shall, from
time to time, in consultation with any senior executive branch officials
the Secretary deems appropriate, review the status of such imports with
respect to the national security.  The Secretary shall inform me of any
circumstances that, in the Secretary’s opinion, might indicate the need
for further action by the President under section 232. 

(4)  Nothing in this proclamation modifies the tariffs imposed pursuant
to Proclamation 9704, as amended; Proclamation 9705, as amended; and
Proclamation 10962, as amended, or any other tariffs imposed pursuant to
section 232.

(5)  Any provision of previous proclamations and Executive Orders that
is inconsistent with this proclamation is superseded to the extent of
such inconsistency.  If any provision of this proclamation or the
application of any provision of this proclamation to any individual or
circumstance is held to be invalid, the remainder of this proclamation
and the application of its provisions to any other individual or
circumstance shall not be affected. 

IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand this twenty-second day
of October, in the year of our Lord two thousand twenty-six, and of the
Independence of the United States of America the two hundred and
fifty-first.

                               DONALD J. TRUMP
