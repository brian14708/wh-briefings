Executive Order 14432

By the authority vested in me as President by the Constitution and the
laws of the United States of America, it is hereby ordered:

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Section</span>
<span style="text-decoration: underline">1</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">Purpose</span>.  For
generations, Americans seeking essential services from the Federal
Government have been required to navigate a system organized around the
structure of Government rather than the needs of the people.  Americans
must search across numerous executive departments and agency (agency)
websites, interpret complicated Government terminology, frequently
provide the same information, and repeatedly prove their identities to a
Government that may already possess the information necessary to serve
them.  The American people deserve better.

Advances in technology now make possible a fundamentally different
relationship between the people and the Federal Government, defined by
simplicity, speed, accuracy, security, and respect for the individual.
 The same spirit of American innovation that can accelerate scientific
discovery can also help Americans complete essential interactions with
the Federal Government.

This order therefore directs the establishment of America.gov as the
unified digital front door to the Federal Government for every
individual in the United States seeking Federal information or services.
 America.gov will not be merely another website, search engine, or
directory of Government links.  It will be a secure, intelligent, and
service-oriented point of entry through which an individual may sign in,
communicate in plain language, receive accurate answers, and, where
authorized and technically available, complete Government transactions
without being required to navigate the websites of multiple agencies.
 America.gov will enable the Federal Government to do its own paperwork
so that the American people may get on with their lives.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">2</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">Policy</span>.  It is the
policy of the United States to:

\(a\)  provide every individual in the United States seeking Federal
information or services with a single, secure, conversational point of
entry through which the citizen may discover, access, and, where
authorized, complete covered Federal transactions;

\(b\)  establish common standards of quality, simplicity, accessibility,
interoperability, security, reliability, and design across participating
Federal digital services and public-facing websites;

\(c\)  preserve each agency’s custody and control of its records,
systems, statutory responsibilities, and adjudicatory authority and
ensure that unifying access to Federal services does not create a
centralized Federal system of records concerning the American people;

\(d\)  protect personal information through data minimization, secure
authentication, auditable authorization, and disclosure practices
consistent with applicable law;

\(e\)  ensure that super intelligence used in connection with
America.gov is accurate, reliable, and transparent; and

\(f\)  preserve existing lawful methods by which Americans may obtain
Federal services, including in-person, telephone, mail, and
agency-specific digital services, so that America.gov provides a better
option but does not become the only option.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">3</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">Definition</span>.  The term
“covered services” means public-facing Federal services that serve more
than 100,000 users in a 12-month period and can be accessed or applied
for online. The term excludes tax filing services provided by the
Internal Revenue Service and services provided by the Department of War
and elements of the Intelligence Community.  The Director of the Office
of Management and Budget (OMB) may, by memorandum, add or exclude
“covered services.”

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">4</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">Establishing
America.gov</span>.  The Administrator of General Services, in
coordination with the Administrator of the National Design Studio and
the Director of OMB, shall:

\(a\) establish and operate America.gov as the single point of entry for
Americans to access covered services online; and

\(b\) take such steps as are necessary to integrate Login.gov with
America.gov, including using Login.gov as the authentication service for
America.gov.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">5</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">Implementing
America.gov</span>.  (a)  Within 90 days of the date of this order, the
Director of OMB shall issue a memorandum to the heads of agencies on
implementation of this order.

\(b\)  Heads of agencies shall:

(i)    identify covered services in their respective agencies;

(ii)   integrate the provisions of such services with America.gov in a
secure and privacy-preserving manner, including by making the public
Application Programming Interfaces, dashboards, and digital forms that
already support services accessible through the America.gov platform;

\(iii\)  complete integration with Login.gov consistent with the
requirements of section 1523(b)(1)(D) of title 6, United States Code,
and OMB Memorandum M-26-18; and

\(iv\)   provide, on an ongoing basis, the Administrator of General
Services and the Director of OMB with historical and current usage and
performance data for covered services.

<span style="text-decoration: underline">Sec</span>.
<span style="text-decoration: underline">6</span>.
 <span style="text-decoration: underline">General Provisions</span>.
 (a)  Nothing in this order shall be construed to impair or otherwise
affect:

\(i\)   the authority granted by law to an executive department or
agency, or the head thereof; or

\(ii\)  the functions of the Director of the Office of Management and
Budget relating to budgetary, administrative, or legislative proposals.

\(b\)  This order shall be implemented consistent with applicable law
and subject to the availability of appropriations.

\(c\)  This order is not intended to, and does not, create any right or
benefit, substantive or procedural, enforceable at law or in equity by
any party against the United States, its departments, agencies, or
entities, its officers, employees, or agents, or any other person.

\(d\)  Nothing in this order shall be construed to require the
disclosure of information that is prohibited by law, or to authorize any
person other than the originating agency to access records maintained by
that agency about an individual, except as provided by the Privacy Act
of 1974 (5 U.S.C. 552a) or other applicable law.

(e)  The costs for publication of this order shall be borne by the
General Services Administration.

                             DONALD J. TRUMP

THE WHITE HOUSE,

    September 29, 2026.
