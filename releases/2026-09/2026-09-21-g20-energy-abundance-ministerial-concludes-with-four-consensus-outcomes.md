title: G20 Energy Abundance Ministerial Concludes with Four Consensus Outcomes
---
 
On Wednesday, September 16<sup>th</sup>, 2026, G20 Energy Ministers
concluded the Energy Abundance Ministerial in Houston, Texas. The
Ministerial was hosted by the White House National Energy Dominance
Council (NEDC), the Department of the Interior, the Department of
Energy, and the Environmental Protection Agency.

At the conclusion of the Ministerial, G20 Ministers reached consensus on
four outcomes advancing energy access, infrastructure development, water
resilience, and supply chain security. The outcomes advance the Energy
Abundance Working Group’s three pillars: Energy Security and Affordable
Baseload, Permitting and Regulatory Efficiency, and Critical Minerals
and Resilient Supply Chains. Together, they support the common sense
solutions that President Trump has championed to increase energy
availability, speed the development of critical infrastructure,
strengthen water resilience, and reduce vulnerabilities in the supply
chains essential to modern economies.

Ministers reached consensus on deliverables that support affordable,
scalable, and context-specific pathways to expand household energy
access, including the use of **[liquefied petroleum gas, or
LPG](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/1-G20-Energy-Ministers-Statement-on-Advancing-Clean-Cooking-Access.pdf)**,
where it is a reliable and scalable means of rapidly displacing
traditional biomass. They also reached agreement on **[Permitting and
Regulatory
Efficiency](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2-G20-EAWG-Permitting-and-Regulatory-Efficiency.pdf)**
to encourage more efficient, predictable, and effective domestic
permitting and regulatory processes, and a **[Water Reuse
Initiative](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/3-G20-EAWG-Water-Reuse-Initiative.pdf)**
outlining voluntary, fit-for-purpose water reuse to strengthen
resilience for communities, energy production, manufacturing,
agriculture, and other critical sectors. Finally, Ministers agreed in
the **[Critical Minerals
Statement](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/4-G20-EAWG-Critical-Minerals-Statement.pdf)**
on voluntary cooperation to build resilient, affordable, secure, and
diversified critical minerals value chains through information sharing,
innovation, investment, capacity building, and local value addition.

“The world cannot build secure energy systems, advanced manufacturing
capacity, or digital infrastructure on fragile mineral supply chains,”
said Secretary of the Interior Doug Burgum, Chair of the National Energy
Dominance Council. “The consensus reached in Houston supports a common
sense approach: diversify supply, strengthen domestic and regional
capacity, encourage investment and innovation, and respect each nation’s
sovereignty over its natural resources. These steps will help reduce
overreliance on any single supplier while creating more durable supply
chains and greater economic opportunity.”

“Energy is the essential ingredient that enables everything we do,” said
Secretary of Energy Chris Wright. “A highly energized society brings
health, wealth, and opportunity. The consensus reached in Houston
advances cooperation to expand the affordable, reliable, and secure
energy, infrastructure, and supply chains needed to support prosperity,
resilience, and opportunity throughout the world.”

“President Trump’s energy abundance agenda reflects reality – strong
environmental stewardship and economic prosperity go hand in hand,” said
Environmental Protection Agency Administrator Lee Zeldin. “The consensus
reached in Houston advances solutions that streamline processes without
sacrificing safeguards, expand water resilience, and enable the
infrastructure needed to deliver reliable and affordable energy. This is
how the G20 can support growth, protect communities, and meet the
demands of the future.”

The investments and agreements announced in Houston demonstrate how
President Trump’s energy abundance agenda is creating jobs, delivering
economic opportunity, strengthening American manufacturing, and
expanding the energy infrastructure needed to power the future. The
Japanese company Hitachi Energy announced a $528 million investment to
build a new power transformer manufacturing facility in Gallman,
Mississippi, expected to create 654 new jobs. Houston-based Caturus
announced a five-train expansion of its Commonwealth LNG export facility
in Louisiana that would add 7.75 million tonnes per annum of capacity
and nearly double the project’s planned capacity to approximately 17.25
million tonnes per annum, Continental Resources signed a memorandum of
understanding with Venezuela’s PDVSA to develop and operate the
126,000-acre Ayacucho 2 Block in the Orinoco Belt, which Continental
estimates contains approximately 30 billion barrels of oil.

At the Ministerial, Administrator Zeldin announced the EPA’s
[repeal](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-finalizes-repeal-2024-power-plant-regulations-delivering-300-billion-savings)
of the majority of the Biden Administration’s greenhouse gas
requirements for U.S. power plants, the largest power sector
deregulatory action in American history. EPA projects the repeal will
save $310 billion and help unleash America’s vast energy resources,
including coal and natural gas. The Administrator also proposed to
repeal the remaining power sector standards, which would save an
additional $370 million in direct compliance costs. Together, these
actions will restore reliable, affordable baseload power and strengthen
American energy dominance.

Representatives from the following nations and unions were present at
the three-day Ministerial: the African Union, the Argentine Republic,
the Commonwealth of Australia, the Federative Republic of Brazil,
Canada, the People’s Republic of China, the European Union, the French
Republic, the Federal Republic of Germany, the Republic of India, the
Republic of Indonesia, the Italian Republic, Japan, the United Mexican
States, the Republic of Poland, the Russian Federation, the Kingdom of
Saudi Arabia, the Republic of Korea, the Republic of Türkiye, and the
United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland.
