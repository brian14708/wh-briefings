title: Memorandum of Understanding Between The Government of the United States of
  America and the Government of Finland Regarding the Technology Prosperity Deal

The Government of the United States of America and the Government of
Finland (hereinafter referred to as the “Participants”),

Expressing mutual interest in science and technology capabilities and
standards to usher in the next Golden Age of Innovation to fortify
freedom and prosperity for generations to come,

Affirming the value of bilateral science and technology collaboration to
enrich the lives and livelihoods of citizens in both countries, and to
elevate the U.S.-Finland Alliance to a higher level,

Building on the longstanding collaboration established under the
existing Agreement Relating to Scientific and Technological Cooperation
between the Government of the United States of America and the
Government of the Republic of Finland, done at Washington May 16, 1995,

Taking note of the Joint Statement of the United States and Finland on
Cooperation in Quantum Information Science and Technology and also the
Joint Statement of the United States and Finland on Cooperation in
Advanced Wireless Communications, and

Recognizing the importance of deepening ties with strategic partners as
a means to strengthen stability, security, and competitiveness,

Have reached the following understandings:

## I. Purpose

The purpose of this Memorandum of Understanding (hereinafter “MOU”) is
to enable collaboration towards joint opportunities of mutual interest
in strategic science and technology disciplines with a focus on
strengthening industrial cooperation and accelerating the development,
deployment, and adoption of emerging technologies to power the next
generation of global connectivity, secure quantum advantage, advance
energy leadership, develop advanced semiconductors, strengthen space
collaboration, advance an open and secure super intelligence (SI) stack,
accelerate secure biotechnology innovation, strengthen research and
industrial security, and advance shipbuilding modernization and Arctic
technology
cooperation.[<sup>\[1\]</sup>](//6096A0EF-A70B-444D-9573-588CCC52C37C#_ftn1)

## II. Areas of Cooperation

The Participants aim to collaborate in a number of disciplines,
including but not limited to the following:

### Accelerating Development and Diffusion of the Trusted Technology Stack in SI and Connectivity

Recognizing the importance of secure and trusted technology
infrastructure to economic prosperity and national security, the
Participants intend to deepen cooperation to advance next-generation SI
and network technologies. The Participants intend to collaborate on
telecommunications R&D, promote and finance the export of trusted
technology stacks, and coordinate on international standards
development.

Focus areas for collaboration are intended to include:

- Promoting development and exports across the full SI and network
  technology stack, including hardware, models, software, applications,
  network equipment, maintenance, long-term customer support, and
  related standards;
- Partnering to finance trusted technology stack exports and to provide
  customer support to accelerate adoption in third-party countries by
  coordinating, as appropriate, applicable U.S. and Finnish financing
  and commercial diplomacy tools to support competitive deployments in
  priority third-country markets;
- Shaping telecommunications standards in line with shared priorities
  through coordinated engagement in international standards bodies
  including the International Telecommunication Union, the Global
  Coalition on Telecommunications, and partnership with industry at
  3GPP;
- Collaborating to shape resolutions in advance of all major ITU
  decision-making conferences, including the 2027 World
  Radiocommunication Conference and 2026 ITU Plenipotentiary;
- Strengthening existing research and development in 5G and 6G and
  next-generation communication technologies through accelerated
  pathways from research and development to commercial and industrial
  deployment, and strengthened industrial cooperation; and
- Building trusted, secure, interoperable 5G and 6G supply chains and
  advocating for policies that accelerate adoption of trusted
  connectivity infrastructure.

### Securing Quantum Advantage

The Participants recognize that quantum technologies are poised to
deliver transformative advances across economic and national security
domains. Sustained leadership in quantum innovation depends on secure
supply chains, strong research and industrial ecosystems, and close
collaboration between trusted partners. The Participants intend to
deepen cooperation to accelerate the development, commercialization, and
responsible deployment of quantum technologies in ways that enhance
prosperity and security.

Focus areas for collaboration are intended to include:

- Collaborating through leading quantum institutions and national
  laboratories to assess quantum performance, develop quantum
  algorithms, and enable scientific discovery, including by advancing
  hybrid quantum-classical computing;
- Accelerating commercial deployment of cryogenics, precision
  instrumentation and semiconductor process engineering firms through
  regulatory harmonization efforts and targeted investments in Finland
  and in the United States;
- Supporting the development of the next generation of scientists and
  engineers through joint research and investments into quantum
  manufacturing;
- Aligning efforts to secure technology and supply chains and to build a
  trusted ecosystem for the research, development, and adoption of new
  quantum technologies;
- Strengthening pathways for information sharing and establishing
  commercial pipelines between U.S. and Finnish stakeholders to
  accelerate the commercialization of quantum technologies and bolster
  the alignment between the two commercial quantum ecosystems;
- Cooperating on trusted quantum ecosystem development by aligning
  efforts focused on secure quantum supply chains and informing
  coordinated engagement in Quantum Development Group (QDG),
  Multilateral Dialogue on Quantum (MDQ), and NATO Transatlantic Quantum
  Community (TQC) engagement;
- Advancing trusted, interoperable standards for quantum technologies
  through existing complementary multilateral and bilateral
  pre-standardization activities as relevant in quantum computing,
  sensing, and networking; and
- Endorsing and advocating for the adoption of Post-Quantum
  Cryptographic standards and supporting the migration of systems to
  those standards, as well as advancing R&D and commercial cooperation
  in encryption technology capabilities and strengthening trade and
  exports in the sector by cyber security companies.

### Unlocking Energy Innovation and Resilience

The Participants are strengthening cooperation to advance secure,
resilient and diversified energy systems. Together we are expanding
energy collaboration across SMR development and deployment, nuclear
fission R&D, geothermal, and energy resilience, to support innovation
and the reliable deployment of next-generation energy infrastructure.

Focus areas for collaboration are intended to include:

- Strengthening civil nuclear energy cooperation to accelerate the
  development and commercial deployment of American and Finnish nuclear
  reactors, including small modular reactors (SMRs), and expand
  collaboration on regulatory harmonization;
- Advancing cooperation on nuclear lifecycle innovation including
  repository engineering, encapsulation of used nuclear fuel, nuclear
  monitoring technologies and SI-enabled geologic modeling;
- Advancing collaboration on geothermal technologies including the
  research, development and deployment of advanced drilling, subsurface
  characterization, and enhanced geothermal systems;
- Advancing development of the secure supply of advanced energy systems,
  including bulk power components and energy storage devices;
- Advancing cooperation on advanced manufacturing including
  rare-earth-free magnets, high-efficiency motors, advanced batteries,
  and advanced materials;
- Advancing cooperation on fusion technologies such as fusion fuel cycle
  and blanket integration systems, neutronics modeling, and fusion
  materials; and
- Promoting innovation in critical minerals technologies including the
  sensing, characterization, extraction, processing and recovery of
  critical minerals and advanced materials essential to energy systems.

### Strengthening Space Collaboration

Recognizing that bilateral cooperation on space benefits both countries’
national interests, the Participants intend to deepen cooperation and
engage in dialogue on space security, space exploration, and space
technology research, development, and commercialization.

Focus areas for collaboration are intended to include:

- Continuing the partnership on space science and exploration, including
  potential collaboration on future Artemis lunar surface exploration
  missions and beyond;
- Facilitating cooperation between commercial actors from the United
  States and Finland, supporting growth of space companies from both
  countries and building on complementary strengths;
- Facilitating commercial and civil space cooperation by addressing
  regulatory burdens on commercial space activities and advocating for
  policies that can adapt to technical development, business innovation,
  and market demands; and
- Leveraging our geostrategic locations as space nations to promote
  Arctic security and prosperity.

### Advancing Defense Innovation

Building on the above-mentioned areas with dual-use applications and the
existing defense agreements, the Participants intend to pursue joint
research projects, conduct pilot activities, and explore pathways for
scaling promising technologies from research and demonstration to
operational and industrial deployment.

### Advancing Biomedical, Biotechnological, and Advanced Material Innovation

Recognizing the importance of advancing biotechnological research,
including in biomedical and biomanufacturing fields, accelerating
materials innovation through super intelligence, and securing
biotechnology and health supply chains, the Participants intend to:

- Leverage insights derived from nationally governed health and research
  data infrastructures to advance precision medicine research and
  clinical research readiness, while reinforcing shared commitments to
  research and industrial security, trusted data infrastructure, and
  resilience against adversary-linked dependencies;
- Strengthen research and development and accelerate innovation and
  adoption of new solutions rooted in biotechnology broadly across
  supply chains and ecosystems; and
- Enhance research that combines computational, experimental, and
  data-based capabilities to accelerate the discovery, design, and
  validation of new materials.

### Advancing Arctic Technology and Operational Capability

Recognizing the strategic importance of the Arctic to shared security,
prosperity, and resilience, the Participants intend to support close
collaboration on Arctic operations, ice navigation, maritime domain
awareness, cold-weather testing, and enabling technologies. Recognizing
Finland’s established leadership in icebreaker design and construction,
the Participants intend to collaborate on icebreaker modernization
efforts and related shipyard and workforce development.

The Participants intend to accelerate industrial cooperation and to
modernize the United States’ icebreaker fleet under ICE Pact, leveraging
Finnish expertise to support the secure and timely design, construction,
and delivery of next-generation Arctic Security Cutters (ASCs).
Expanding this framework, the Participants intend to explore
possibilities to deepen their collaboration in ship design and
shipbuilding technologies, processes, and shipyard management.
Furthermore, the Participants intend to jointly invest in enabling
technologies such as SI-driven manufacturing, ice technology, unmanned
systems, and space-enabled situational awareness.

The Participants recognize the strategic importance of advancing Arctic
connectivity including both satellite and submarine communication
services. The Participants intend to advocate policies and commercial
cooperation that enhance satellite coverage at high latitudes and
support the establishment of subsea communication cables in the Arctic
region, thereby strengthening connectivity between North America and
Northern Europe and, eventually, the Indo-Pacific region.

### Strengthening Security in Research and Industry

Recognizing the importance of shared research integrity and security
across critical and emerging technology research and development, the
Participants intend to strengthen continued collaboration to protect
their technologies, their critical supply chains, and their people. The
Participants intend to collaborate on research and industry security,
and to identify and mitigate threats to both the research enterprise and
private sector research and development.

Focus areas for collaboration are intended to include:

- Strengthening security in research and industry through intellectual
  property security, investment screening, and partnerships;
- Building trusted science and innovation ecosystems by mitigating
  high-risk entity participation in sensitive science and technology R&D
  activities;
- Strengthening safeguards and enforcement mechanisms for
  government-funded research and talent programs by enhancing disclosure
  of foreign funding and affiliations in order to mitigate and address
  security risks related to partnerships with entities of shared
  security concerns;
- Establishing a bilateral research security dialogue and information
  exchange on threats, cases, risk assessments, mitigation efforts, and
  entities of security concern;
- Collaborating with allies and partners to adopt enhanced research
  security practices to facilitate a trusted innovation ecosystem;
- Enhancing transparency and due diligence across the innovation
  ecosystem by strengthening vetting of research institutions,
  researchers, partnerships, and funding and implementing safeguards
  against foreign interference and malign talent recruitment; and
- Applying shared research integrity and security principles across TPD
  workstreams to guide collaboration, protect sensitive R&D activities,
  and inform future research and industrial security programming.

## III. Operation and Discontinuation

1.   This MOU becomes operative on the date of the last signature. The
Participants may modify this MOU by written mutual decision. Either
Participant may discontinue this MOU at any time by providing written
notice of discontinuation to the other Participant. The discontinuation
is expected to commence on a date 180 days after the date on which
notice of discontinuation is delivered. The Participants intend to
advance the implementation of the MOU through the Joint Committee
Meeting mechanism. The Participants intend to leverage, as appropriate,
the Science and Technology Agreement as an operational framework for
implementation of this MOU.

2.   This MOU does not constitute or create and is not intended to
constitute or create any legally binding obligations. Nothing in this
MOU is intended to alter or affect any existing agreements between the
Participants.

3.   Cooperation under this MOU is intended to take place in accordance
with the Participants’ applicable national legislation and international
obligations, including, for Finland, applicable current and future
European Union coordination, laws, and regulations, and without
prejudice to the Participants’ respective regulatory processes.

4.   Nothing in this MOU commits the Participants to the expenditure of
funds.

5.   Activities involving personal data are intended to comply with
applicable data-protection frameworks. Nothing in this MOU authorizes
the disclosure or transfer of classified, controlled, confidential
business, or proprietary information. Matters involving the ownership,
protection, access, use, licensing, or commercialization of intellectual
property are expected to be addressed through separate implementing
arrangements or project-specific agreements, as appropriate.

6.   The foregoing represents the understanding reached between the
Participants on the matters referred to in this MOU.

Signed in Washington, D.C. on October 29 in English.
