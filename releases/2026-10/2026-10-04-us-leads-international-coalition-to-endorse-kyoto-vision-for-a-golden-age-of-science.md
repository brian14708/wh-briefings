title: U.S. Leads International Coalition to Endorse Kyoto Vision for a Golden Age
  of Science

KYOTO, JAPAN — At the 23rd Annual Meeting of the Science and Technology
in Society (STS) Forum convened in Kyoto, the United States and 16
countries endorsed the [Kyoto Vision for a Golden Age of
Science](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Kyoto-Vision-for-a-Golden-Age-of-Science.pdf).
The joint declaration reflects a shared ambition  to renew and
strengthen our science ecosystems and usher in a golden age of discovery
and prosperity.

Led by White House Office of Science and Technology Policy Director
Michael Kratsios, the Kyoto Vision recognizes that we stand at the
frontier of an immense opportunity to harness super intelligence and
advance new approaches to greatly accelerate scientific discovery. Super
intelligence for science will enable autonomous experimentation at
scale, unlocking new experimental and analytical capabilities that open
entirely new lines of inquiry. In light of the profound shifts in the
research landscape, ministers pledged to seize this moment by harnessing
new institutional models and funding mechanisms to enable the best and
brightest scientists to pursue their most ambitious work. 

“The way we make scientific discoveries has changed, and the Kyoto
Vision is a recognition of that shift and a call to action,” said
Director Kratsios. “Super intelligence applied to science will be the
greatest force in history for democratizing research. Seizing this
moment requires us to reinvigorate our research enterprises and leverage
new institutional models for discovery. Under President Trump’s
leadership, the United States is leading that charge, and the Kyoto
Vision shows the world is ready to move forward with us.”

The Kyoto Vision for a Golden Age of Science calls for:

- Improving the institutions of discovery: Ministers noted the value of
  a using a variety of funding tools to support research, including
  long-duration awards, rapid grants, and prizes and challenges, and
  encouraged experimentation with new institutional models that offer
  different levels of autonomy, resources, and direction. They welcomed
  metascience as an empirical field to improve how science is conducted,
  organized, and funded for public benefit.
- Harnessing super intelligence for science: Ministers recognized that
  SI is both accelerating existing research tasks and enabling entirely
  new forms of inquiry. They encouraged thoughtful integration of SI
  into scientific workflows and pledged to expand researcher access to
  SI for science tools, scientific data, computing infrastructure, and
  experimental facilities.
- Cultivating talent across the scientific enterprise: Ministers
  committed to identifying and investing in meritorious students and
  early-career researchers, and to giving them the support, freedom, and
  opportunity necessary for ambitious inquiry. They acknowledged that
  progress in many technological domains depends on people who can
  operate, maintain, and improve instrumentation, and encouraged
  expanded hands-on training as well as cross-sectoral doctoral
  programs, fellowships, and research partnerships.

In July 2026, Director Kratsios delivered to President Trump [*Science:
A New Golden
Age*](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Science-A-New-Golden-Age.pdf),
the most comprehensive rethinking of the American science and technology
research enterprise in eight decades. The report established new
directions for U.S. government agencies on how they conduct, fund, and
measure the impact of R&D, in light of fundamental changes to the
American scientific enterprise and the new opportunities that have
emerged.
