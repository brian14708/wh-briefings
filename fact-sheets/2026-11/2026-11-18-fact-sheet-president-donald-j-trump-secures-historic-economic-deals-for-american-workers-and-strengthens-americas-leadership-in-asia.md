**  
SUPPORTING AMERICAN JOBS AND STRENGTHENING U.S. DIPLOMATIC LEADERSHIP IN
THE INDO-PACIFIC:**  Today, President Donald J. Trump concluded a
two-day visit to the Philippines for a State Visit and the Association
of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Summit, where he announced over $17
billion in commercial deals and strategic investments for U.S. companies
that will support tens of thousands of American jobs, bolster regional
security that protects the United States from illicit activity, and
support a free and open Indo-Pacific that economically benefits American
companies, workers, and consumers.

**  
SECURING HISTORIC DEALS WITH THE PHILIPPINES:**  President Ferdinand R.
Marcos, Jr. hosted President Trump for a historic State Visit in Manila,
where the two leaders announced landmark deals that will strengthen the
U.S.-Philippine Alliance and support American companies and workers. The
leaders also committed to eliminate the U.S. trade deficit with the
Philippines through increased Philippine imports of American energy and
agriculture products, which will support the jobs of tens of thousands
of American engineers, technicians, and farmers.

- U.S. companies Boeing and GE Aerospace finalized a $12 billion sale to
  Philippine Airlines for up to 20 Boeing 787 aircrafts with GE
  Aerospace engines, supporting 47,000 U.S. jobs. 
- The United States and the Philippines will pursue a historic Foreign
  Military Financing (FMF) loan package of more than $2 billion to
  provide the Philippines with critical defense and security
  capabilities from U.S. suppliers, supporting over 7,000 American jobs
  and advancing the President’s National Security Strategy and America
  First Arms Transfer Strategy, all while protecting returns for the
  American taxpayer.
  - President Trump’s America First Arms Transfer Strategy ensures that
    the surge in allies’ spending benefits the U.S. defense industry,
    reindustrializing America and supporting American jobs while arming
    our partners with the best capabilities in the world.
- The United States will utilize $400 million in FMF for the Philippine
  military to expand its operational reach and deterrence capabilities
  in the South China Sea, including long-range fires, command and
  control, critical infrastructure, and multi-domain drones, supporting
  1,500 American jobs. 
- The United States and the Philippines agreed to partner on supply the
  Philippines’ sovereign cloud infrastructure with trusted U.S. Super
  Intelligence technology through the United States’ SI Exports Program.
  - The Philippine Department of National Defense (DND) announced a $37
    million contract with a U.S. firm, supporting 140 American
    jobs.  The contract delivers SI-enabled mission support, expands
    U.S.-Philippine defense cooperation, and improves interoperability. 
  - The Philippine DND committed to work with trusted U.S. technology
    providers on a $63 million initiative to modernize its defense cloud
    infrastructure for SI use cases, supporting over 200 American jobs. 
- The United States and the Philippines committed to improving
  cooperation in critical technologies. 
  - The United States and the Philippines announced exploration of an
    intergovernmental agreement on civil-nuclear technology which
    would open opportunities for U.S. nuclear companies, support
    American jobs, and generate gigawatts of energy for the Philippines.
    This marks the first U.S. civil-nuclear intergovernmental agreement
    with an Indo-Pacific partner.

  <!-- -->

  - U.S. company Astranis announced it is offering connectivity services
    from its U.S.-manufactured AGILA satellite to the Philippines, a
    commercial opportunity worth up to $200 million, supporting 600
    American jobs, to provide independent and secure communications
    infrastructure for the Philippine government and people.
- President Trump is leveraging strategic infrastructure to strengthen
  the U.S.-Philippine Alliance, secure critical supply chains, open new
  markets for American businesses, and support American workers.
  - Subic Grain will build a new $40 million fuel storage depot at Subic
    Bay to support U.S. military and commercial operations. 
  - The United States and the Philippines announced that the Philippines
    will modernize its Basco Airport to improve U.S.-Philippine
    operational access, disaster response, and commercial supply chains
    in the Philippines’ northernmost islands. 

**  
FINALIZING THE U.S.-THAILAND TRADE DEAL AND INCREASING MILITARY
SALES:**  At the ASEAN Summit, President Trump announced the substantial
conclusion of a trade deal and a weapons sale with Thailand that will
give American workers, manufacturers, farmers, ranchers, and businesses
unprecedented access to Thailand’s market.  

- The trade deal, once signed, will eliminate tariffs on 99 percent of
  goods and remove non-tariff barriers on American vehicles, medical
  devices, pharmaceuticals, ethanol, and poultry, increasing market
  access for a full range of U.S. industrial and agricultural exports.

<!-- -->

- The trade deal will also protect and enforce U.S. intellectual
  property, increase economic security alignment, and strengthen and
  enforce Thai labor and environmental laws to balance trade.
- Separately, Thailand intends to commit up to $123 million toward the
  purchase of America’s High Mobility Artillery Rocket Systems
  (HIMARS).  The sale would give America’s treaty Ally long-range
  precision fires and support 400 American jobs and the U.S. defense
  industrial base. Once concluded, it would be the first deal of its
  kind in mainland Southeast Asia. 

**  
SECURING INVESTMENT DEALS ACROSS SOUTHEAST ASIA:  **President Trump
opened new markets for American goods and created opportunities for U.S.
companies, furthering U.S. leadership in digital connectivity across the
region.

- In Indonesia, U.S. company Latitude Energy and Indonesian sovereign
  wealth fund Danantara announced negotiations on a $2 billion project
  that will use world-class U.S. technology to improve Indonesia’s
  utilization of its coal resources. 
- Panasonic signed a multi-year agreement to buy advanced silicon-carbon
  anode materials from U.S. company Sila.  The agreement is worth up to
  $1 billion and will support an additional 500 American jobs.
- In Malaysia, Navajo Transitional Energy Company (NTEC) signed a
  three-year $100 million agreement to supply coal from its southern
  Montana mine to TNB Fuel Services, the fuel procurement subsidiary of
  Malaysia’s national power utility, Tenaga Nasional Berhad.

**  
LEADING THE REGION IN SUPER INTELLIGENCE:**  In addition to Philippine
commitments on American SI technology above, President Trump announced
further deployment of American SI technology across the region through
our SI Exports Program, cementing U.S. firms and American technology as
the global standard for trusted partners.

- President Trump and Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar announced that a
  U.S. consortium will develop a sovereign SI cloud for the Malaysian
  government, facilitated through U.S.-ASEAN SI SPARK.  
- Brunei’s Unified National Networks intends to award U.S. company
  Vertiv the Rimba Data Centre tender and source the nearly $1 billion
  buildout from U.S. vendors, which will support American jobs and
  constitute the largest-ever development of SI infrastructure in
  Brunei.
