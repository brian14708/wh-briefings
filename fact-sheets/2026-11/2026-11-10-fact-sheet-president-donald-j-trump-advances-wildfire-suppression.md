**KEEPING AMERICANS SAFE:** Today, President Donald J. Trump signed a
Presidential Memorandum to protect communities at risk from wildfires
and ensure immediate action is taken to control fires before they become
major natural disasters that destroy thousands of acres of timber and
imperil critical water resources and watersheds.

- The Memorandum determines that it is in the paramount interest of the
  United States to exempt federal firefighting activities, including the
  aerial use, deployment, and discharge of fire retardant, from the
  Clean Water Act permitting. 
- The Memorandum exempts federal firefighting activities from the Clean
  Water Act to ensure wildland firefighters have the necessary tools to
  protect America’s rural communities and natural resources from
  unpredictable and difficult-to-control wildfires during the 2027 fire
  season despite an adverse district court decision. 

**GUARANTEEING FIRE SUPPRESSION TOOLS: **To prepare for the upcoming
wildland fire season, President Trump is ensuring that Americans and the
federal government are well-equipped to control wildfires.

- Wildfire is a serious national threat to our forests and rangelands
  and our Forest Service must be equipped to respond to unpredictable
  wildfires.
- Protracted litigation created legal uncertainty and regulatory
  requirements that have tied the hands of those who put their lives at
  risk fighting wildfire under the most difficult conditions. 
- Under an adverse district court decision, use of aerial fire retardant
  in federal wildland firefighting efforts now requires a Clean Water
  Act permit, however, despite numerous efforts, the U.S. Forest Service
  has been unsuccessful in securing a permit due to significant
  regulatory hurdles. 
- This Memorandum fulfills the permitting requirements for a year,
  ensuring the judicious use of aerial fire retardant will protect
  lives, livelihoods, communities, infrastructure and resources. 

**STRENGTHENING WILDFIRE PREVENTION AND RESPONSE: **This Memorandum
expands on a series of significant actions the President has taken to
protect the nation’s forests, rangelands, and recreational landscapes
from wildfires for generations to come.

- In his first term, President Trump signed legislation to improve the
  funding process for the U.S. Forest Service and the Department of the
  Interior’s wildfire suppression operations.
  - Before this fix, the funding structure forced wildland firefighting
    agencies to take funds from prevention programs to cover the rising
    costs of responding to wildfires.
- In March 2025, President Trump signed an Executive Order immediately
  increasing domestic timber production and streamlining federal
  policies to enhance forest management, reduce wildfire risks, and
  decrease reliance on imported timber.
- In March 2025, President Trump signed an Executive Order empowering
  states, localities, and citizens to more effectively prepare for
  incidents like cyber attacks, wildfire, and weather events.
  - From that, in June 2026, the White House released its National
    Resilience Strategy, establishing a risk-informed posture driven by
    a return to federalism, tech-infused modernization, and simplified
    governance.
- In June 2025, President Trump signed an Executive Order returning
  common sense to wildfire prevention and response.
- In January 2026, President Trump signed an Executive Order cutting
  through bureaucratic red tape and speeding up reconstruction in the
  Pacific Palisades and Eaton Canyon areas after devastating wildfires
  destroyed nearly 40,000 acres of homes and businesses.
