**IMPROVING WATER QUALITY OF THE CHESAPEAKE BAY:** Today, President
Donald J. Trump signed an Executive Order to remove unnecessary
administrative costs and direct federal resources for the Chesapeake Bay
toward projects that improve water quality, promoting a more efficient,
accountable, and results-driven effort to improve the Chesapeake Bay.

- The Order rescinds the outdated and misguided Obama Administration
  2009 Executive Order 13508, withdrawing its vague and confusing
  federal requirements that have been leveraged in support of stormwater
  management fees, or “rain taxes,” imposed by States or localities that
  unnecessarily raise costs for American households.
- The Order directs the Secretary of Agriculture, the Secretary of
  Commerce, the Secretary of Homeland Security, the Secretary of the
  Interior, the Secretary of War, and the Administrator of the
  Environmental Protection Agency to focus resources provided for the
  health of the Chesapeake Bay away from activities that do not create
  measurable progress towards the stated goals of the Chesapeake Bay
  Watershed Agreement, and towards direct, on-the-ground projects, in
  areas of highest need that would result in the most impactful water
  quality improvements.
- The Order directs the Environmental Protection Agency to coordinate
  with the jurisdictions of the Chesapeake Bay watershed to assess the
  financial burdens placed on their residents and take measures to
  encourage these jurisdictions to effectuate this Executive Order and
  promote the environmental health of the Chesapeake Bay without
  increasing costs on individual residents of these jurisdictions.
- The Order ensures taxpayer dollars are used efficiently by
  consolidating and cutting unnecessary administrative costs while
  improving the work toward Chesapeake Bay Restoration goals.

**SUPPORTING CLEAN WATER WITHOUT INCREASED COSTS: **The** **Trump
Administration is committed to supporting a healthy Chesapeake Bay
without burdensome and costly “rain taxes.”

- The Chesapeake Bay is the largest estuary in the United States and
  serves a critically important economic purpose to the communities it
  directly and indirectly touches. 
- Federal, State, and local government initiatives and programs have
  significantly improved the water quality of the Chesapeake Bay over
  the years, however future actions and best practices are needed to
  continue promoting the economic health and well-being of the
  communities the Chesapeake Bay serves.
- State and local governments have erroneously relied on the outdated
  Executive Order’s broad language that mandated States and
  municipalities contribute to improving the environmental health of the
  Chesapeake Bay to impose on American households and small
  businesses hundreds of dollars annually in stormwater management fees,
  commonly referred to as “rain taxes.”
- Federal programs and resources for protecting the health of the
  Chesapeake Bay need to appropriately prioritize and focus on projects
  that will result in the most direct and impactful water quality
  improvements for the Bay.
  - With continued investment and improved coordination, the Federal
    government will better complement and support effective State,
    local, and private sector investments, resulting in the most
    targeted and efficient use of taxpayer dollars. 

**IMPROVING ENVIRONMENTAL STANDARDS AND LOWERING COSTS FOR
AMERICANS:*** *President Trump has taken action to protect Americans’
pocketbooks while ensuring Americans have clean air and water.

- The Environmental Protection Agency has reduced burdens on states,
  strengthened oversight and accountability, and prioritized
  regenerative agricultural best management practices and other projects
  that directly reduce nutrient and sediment runoff. 
- In Fiscal Year 2026, the Environmental Protection Agency streamlined
  the Chesapeake Bay Program, consolidated assistance agreements,
  reduced overhead and restructured future funding opportunities to
  reduce annual administrative spending by nearly $1 million and ensure
  that more funding supports direct, on-the-ground projects that reduce
  nutrient and sediment runoff into the Chesapeake Bay and its
  tributaries.
  - From these efforts, the Environmental Protection Agency awarded a
    historic level of funding to the six watershed states and the
    District of Columbia—$2.7 million more than in Fiscal Year 2025—and
    positioned the program for further increased support for projects
    that directly improve Chesapeake Bay water quality.
- The Trump Administration has taken a number of other actions to reduce
  the cost of living while maintaining appropriate environmental
  standards. 
  - In December 2025, President Trump delivered major relief to American
    families by resetting the Biden Administration’s costly and unlawful
    Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) standards to levels that can
    actually be met with conventional gasoline and diesel vehicles,
    averting a $1,000 increase in the average cost of a new vehicle and
    preserving affordable options for American drivers.

  <!-- -->

  - In February 2026, President Trump announced the single largest
    deregulatory action in American history, saving Americans $1.3
    trillion: the full revocation of the disastrous Obama-era
    “Endangerment Finding” and the consumer mandates that depended on
    it.

  <!-- -->

  - In February 2026, President Trump’s Environmental Protection Agency
    released guidance affirming farmers’ ability to lawfully fix their
    own agricultural and non-road equipment, saving an estimated $33,000
    per repair on average, and in June 2026, President Trump expanded
    Americans’ freedom to fix their own vehicles.
- This effective stewardship is cutting administrative costs, improving
  accountability, and putting more federal funding where it can deliver
  meaningful water-quality results for the Chesapeake Bay and its
  tributaries.
- Thanks to President Trump’s action today, accountability and
  transparency will guide every funding decision, ensuring meaningful
  results for the Bay’s ecosystem and communities.
