**ENDING CANADA’S UNFAIR ACCESS TO FEDERAL PROCUREMENT: **Today,
President Donald J. Trump signed a Presidential Memorandum to identify
and take steps to remove Canadian-origin goods from Federal Civil
Government Procurement in response to Canada’s measures that have denied
U.S. firms access to Canada’s federal and provincial procurement
markets. 

- The Memorandum directs the Director of the Office of Management and
  Budget and the United States Trade Representative, in coordination
  with the Federal Acquisition Regulatory Council, to identify and take
  all steps toward removing or otherwise making non-available for
  purchase Canadian-origin items in the federal civil procurement
  system.
- The Memorandum also directs the United States Trade Representative to
  monitor Canada’s continued treatment of U.S. origin goods in Canadian
  government procurement. 

**PUTTING AMERICA FIRST: **President Trump is taking action to hold
Canada accountable for its continued unreasonable and discriminatory
treatment of U.S. goods, which has burdened and disadvantaged
hardworking Americans. 

- Canada has received preferential access to the U.S. market for years,
  while doubling down on and continuing its unfair treatment of U.S.
  commerce.  
- Despite Canada’s procurement commitments to the United States,
  Canadian provinces have disadvantaged American small businesses and
  companies by, among other things, establishing preferences for
  Canadian products and content under the “Buy Canadian” policy. 
- Meanwhile, Canadian companies have preferential access to over $280
  billion of the U.S. government procurement system while U.S. companies
  no longer receive equal treatment in the Canadian government
  procurement system.

**PROTECTING AMERICAN WORKERS AND ENSURING FAIR TRADE: **President Trump
is continuing to deliver on his promise to keep American workers,
farmers, and businesses first by ending other countries’ unfair and
unreasonable trade practices against the United States. 

- President Trump’s America First Trade Policy was created to benefit
  American workers and families and has dramatically expanded global
  market access for U.S. exports, strengthened workers’ incomes,
  reshored and supported American jobs, and reduced the trade deficit.
- The United States, under President Trump’s leadership, did not agree
  to renew the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) in its
  current form, because the deal is not sufficiently beneficial for U.S.
  manufacturers, farmers, ranchers, workers, service suppliers, and
  businesses of all sizes. 
- Through negotiations with foreign trading partners and the strategic
  use of tariffs, President Trump has secured trillions in private and
  foreign investment to bring American jobs and manufacturing back to
  the United States while diversifying global supply chains and reducing
  dependence on adversarial nations.
- On September 8, 2026, President Trump banned and increased tariffs on
  certain products under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 to
  address Canada’s continued discrimination against American exporters
  and to offset the disadvantage or burden on U.S. commerce from
  Canada’s discriminatory treatment against U.S. exports of alcoholic
  beverages, dairy, and motor vehicles.
