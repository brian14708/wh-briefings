  Initiatives This Century'

**WASHINGTON, DC **—** **Today,** **at the *Science: A New Golden
Age* Summit, the** **Trump Administration launched the most ambitious
set of science initiatives in decades, representing over $6 billion in
investments across the Federal Government, industry, academia, and
philanthropy to usher in a New Golden Age of Science. 

The Summit builds on [*Science: A New Golden
Age*](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Science-A-New-Golden-Age.pdf),
a report authored by White House Office of Science and Technology Policy
Director Michael Kratsios to President Trump. The report was written in
response to the
President’s [directive](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2025/03/a-letter-to-michael-kratsios-director-of-the-white-house-office-of-science-and-technology-policy/) to
revitalize the American scientific enterprise. Today, America’s largest
Federal research agencies and partners across the science ecosystem
launched whole-of-nation efforts to position the United States to lead
in a new era of scientific discovery. These initiatives will equip
scientists with the tools to harness SI-driven scientific revolution,
support the best and brightest American STEM students and early-career
scientists, revive the pursuit of ambitious national scientific
missions, and encourage the creation of new scientific institutions.

“Today marks the most transformative day for the American scientific
enterprise since the reshaping of the Federal research ecosystem
following World War II. The efforts announced today will channel
billions of dollars across our science ecosystem to realize the new
scientific opportunities that now exist.” said Director Kratsios. “Under
President Trump’s leadership, we are ushering in a New Golden Age of
Science powered by super intelligence, a return to merit, and bold
national missions that will inspire Americans for generations to come.”

The Trump Administration continues to pursue a whole-of-nation approach
to renew American Science for a New Golden Age of Discovery. With
today’s announcements, President Trump is forging new partnerships
across government, industry, academia, and philanthropy to lay the
foundation for the next generation of American scientific leadership.

**EQUIPPING AMERICAN SCIENTISTS WITH SUPER INTELLIGENCE**: Super
intelligence (SI) will be the greatest accelerant of scientific progress
in human history and will transform every scientific discipline.
President Trump continues to prioritize American scientists by ensuring
broad access to the compute and scientific instrumentation needed for
SI-enabled scientific research. 

- The Trump Administration announced $2.4B in SI for science tools and
  compute credits for the Genesis Mission Consortium from eleven
  industry partners, which will support over 15 Federal agencies working
  towards National Science & Technology Challenges.
  - Industry partner commitments include NVIDIA ($1B), AMD ($500M),
    OpenAI ($200M), $150M each from Anthropic and Google, $100M each
    from AMP and Emerald AI, and $50M each from AWS, Armada, Crusoe, and
    Micron. This follows the expansion of the Genesis Mission in July to
    over 15 Federal agencies and a $5 billion
    Federal [commitment](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/07/45502/) to
    support National Science & Technology Challenges across energy,
    health, space, and more.
- The Trump Administration announced historic investments in scientific
  computing through state and regional SI infrastructure hubs, making
  advanced SI resources more widely available to researchers across the
  country. 
  - More than 14 universities across 10 states launched the Southeast
    Regional SI Computing Consortium to build and share leadership-class
    compute infrastructure for scientific research. 

  <!-- -->

  - Universities, industry, and the State of Georgia announced a $1
    billion investment in scientific computing and related workforce
    training efforts for students and faculty.

  <!-- -->

  - Five universities across North and South Carolina joined together to
    launch a regional compute hub, unlocking SI-enabled science for
    researchers across the region.
- The National Science Foundation (NSF) and the Department of Energy
  (DOE) announced an investment of over $100 million in SI-enabled
  scientific instrumentation and autonomous laboratories to increase
  scientific productivity and unlock new modes of research. This follows
  NSF’s
  July [announcement](https://www.nsf.gov/tip/updates/nsf-announces-400m-investment-new-national-network-ai) of
  a $400 million investment in programmable cloud labs to accelerate and
  democratize access to science.

**REFOCUSING ON THE BEST AND BRIGHTEST**: President Trump has replaced
DEI with merit across the Federal R&D enterprise, ensuring
high-potential American STEM students and early-career scientists
receive the resources and support they need early in their careers to
pursue ambitious opportunities at the frontiers of science and
technology.

- The Trump Administration is announcing new merit-based K-12 STEM
  opportunities focused on gifted students and meaningful hands-on
  technical learning. 
  - The Trump Administration revived the NSF Young Scholars Program,
    funded primarily by philanthropy and inexplicably abandoned in 1996,
    to invest in students in grades 3-12 on the basis of demonstrated
    mathematics ability.

  <!-- -->

  - DOE launched the Battelle-Genesis Mission High School Fellowship to
    provide advanced high school students with transformative hands-on
    research experiences at DOE National Laboratories.
- The Trump Administration is strengthening investments in American STEM
  PhD students, ensuring the STEM PhD remains a desirable pathway for
  America’s most talented students to continue their technical training.
  - The Trump Administration launched the Genesis Mission Fellowship, a
    $100 million initiative for students to pursue accelerated four-year
    PhDs with dual competency in super intelligence and another science
    or engineering discipline and research experiences embedded in
    industry and a DOE National Laboratory.

  <!-- -->

  - NSF launched a 6-year Integrated Bachelors-to-PhD program, enabling
    promising students to complete doctoral studies on an accelerated
    timeline, and speeding up their contributions to the American
    scientific enterprise. This builds off of NSF’s
    July [announcement](https://www.nsf.gov/news/nsf-partners-universities-industry-pilot-initiative-four) of
    an innovative accelerated 4-year PhD program co-funded by industry. 
- The Trump Administration launched Generation Discovery, which will
  bring a hundred exceptional early-career scientists into the NIH’s
  Intramural Research Program, enabling early-career researchers to do
  bold, ambitious work at the most pivotal stages in their career. This
  continues NIH’s FY2026 support for the largest ever cohort of
  early-stage investigators in history.

**BRINGING BACK NATIONAL MISSIONS**: From the Manhattan Project to the
Apollo Program, some of America’s greatest scientific achievements have
come from focused national missions that united the Nation’s brightest
minds behind an ambitious common goal. President Trump has brought back
National Missions in super intelligence, quantum, fusion, and space
exploration that will excite Americans for generations to come. 

- NASA and DOE solidified a historic partnership to develop nuclear
  power and space nuclear fuels. NASA and DOE will collaborate to
  design, build, and deploy multiple space systems, including NASA’s
  Space Reactor-1, a nuclear-powered spacecraft to Mars expected to
  launch in 2028, as well as Lunar Reactor-1, the first fission reactor
  on the lunar surface. This MOU will support implementation of
  President Trump’s December 2025 [Executive
  Order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/ensuring-american-space-superiority/) on
  “Ensuring American Space Superiority”
  and [NSTM-3](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/NSTM-3-2026_04_14-corrected.pdf) launching
  a National Initiative for American Space Nuclear Power.
- DOE announced the Quantum Computing Priority Applications, a set of
  eight key problems that will guide the Quantum Genesis Q Competition,
  a $215 million initiative to fund teams to demonstrate the world’s
  first fault-tolerant, scientifically relevant quantum computers. These
  challenges will help achieve the goals set out in President Trump’s
  June 2026 [Executive
  Order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/ushering-in-the-next-frontier-of-quantum-innovation/) “Ushering
  in the Next Frontier of Quantum Innovation.” 
- NSF launched two $33 million Grand Research Challenge (GRC) prize
  competitions in Quantum+X applications and synthetic multicellularity.
  These challenges will facilitate multidisciplinary collaboration,
  attract non-traditional entrants, and form cross-sector teams to spur
  rapid advances in use-inspired basic discovery.
- This week, the NIH, DOE, and Biohub launched a $1.8B virtual biology
  initiative to create the data and modeling foundation for virtual
  cells, which will develop SI models that can better predict how cells
  and biological systems respond to disease and develop potential
  interventions. This initiative will support [Bio
  Genesis](https://www.nih.gov/bio-genesismission), a national mission
  to double the pace of biomedical innovation within the next five to
  ten years.

**BUILDING INSTITUTIONS THAT ACCELERATE DISCOVERY:** President Trump is
creating new institutions of discovery to enable American Scientists to
pursue major scientific and technical objectives that fall outside of
disciplines and conventional funding mechanisms. 

- The Trump Administration is supporting X-Labs across agencies, a new
  generation of independent research organizations that give
  multidisciplinary teams of scientists and engineers the autonomy and
  resources to tackle specific technical challenges.
  - The Trump Administration launched the X-Labs Consortium with over
    $310 million in industry and philanthropic commitments to support
    Federally-funded X-Labs teams with advanced scientific
    instrumentation and facility space. This builds on the $1.5 billion
    NSF
    X-Labs [initiative](https://www.nsf.gov/tip/updates/nsf-announces-15b-nsf-x-labs-initiative-pursue-generational) announced
    in May, which has supported X-Labs across scientific
    instrumentation, quantum systems, physical SI, predictive biology,
    and next-generation computation.  
- The Trump Administration recognizes that metascience, or applying the
  scientific method to evaluate how we fund and conduct science, is
  necessary to evaluate the impact of new experiments and maximize
  return on Federal investments in research.
  - NSF, DOE, NASA, and DOW launched new metascience initiatives to
    inform how they fund and conduct science. Agencies will run
    experiments and analyze data to better identify exceptional ideas,
    empower talented researchers, reduce unnecessary friction, and
    accelerate the pace of discovery.

  <!-- -->

  - The NSF and the United Kingdom Research and Innovation Metascience
    Unit announced metascience cooperation to measure research impact,
    improve research review, and test new tools for science, laying the
    foundation for an International Network of Metascience Units. This
    announcement builds on the U.S.-led [Kyoto
    Vision](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Kyoto-Vision-for-a-Golden-Age-of-Science.pdf) for
    a New Golden Age of Science.
