**FURTHER COUNTERING CANADA’S DISCRIMINATIORY TREATMENT OF AMERICAN
EXPORTS: **Today, to continue addressing Canada’s discriminatory
treatment of U.S. commerce, President Donald J. Trump signed a
Proclamation pursuant to Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 to
further refine the scope of the tariffs on certain Canadian products
previously announced on July 20, 2026 and modified on September 8, 2026.

- After Canada walked away from a near-final trade deal with the United
  States this past summer and imposed new retaliatory tariffs on about
  $20 billion of U.S. exports, President Trump signed five Proclamations
  pursuant to Section 338 to ban certain products from Canada and modify
  the scope of the tariffs on certain Canadian products previously
  announced on July 20, 2026.  
- To continue effectively offsetting the burden to U.S. commerce while
  better serving the public interest, President Trump is further
  modifying Proclamation 11048 (Imposing Additional Duties To Offset
  Canadian Discrimination Against the Commerce of the United States With
  Respect to Motor Vehicles), as amended, by removing certain products —
  such as plywood sheets, some paper products, certain machinery and
  refrigeration equipment, and certain packing crates and boxes — from
  the scope of the Section 338 tariffs and replacing those products with
  certain valves, carpets, waste products, and others.
- These Section 338 tariffs apply to all covered goods regardless of
  whether a good originates under the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement
  (USMCA), and they apply in addition to tariffs imposed under Section
  232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962.
- The product additions and removals will take effect on October 30,
  2026. 

**DEFENDING THE INTERESTS OF AMERICAN WORKERS AND ENSURING FAIR
TRADE:** President Trump is taking measures to address Canada’s
continued and additional retaliation and discrimination against U.S.
commerce to protect American farmers, manufacturers, and workers.

- Section 338 authorizes the President to suspend, revoke, supplement,
  or amend any proclamation under Section 338 whenever the President
  deems that the public interests require such action.
- President Trump is further refining the scope of the July 20 and
  September 8 actions to better serve the public interest, and to ensure
  these measures continue to effectively offset the burden or
  disadvantage to U.S. commerce from Canada’s discriminatory measures.
- The modified Section 338 tariffs will continue to safeguard American
  workers from Canada’s discriminatory or unreasonable and unequal
  practices. 

**CONTINUING AMERICA FIRST TRADE POLICY:** President Trump is using all
the tools at his disposal to restore reciprocity in our bilateral trade
relationships and protect the economic prosperity and national security
of the American people.

- By imposing tariffs to address non-reciprocal foreign trade practices,
  President Trump is incentivizing companies to reshore jobs and
  production lines back to American soil. Recent new investments part of
  $21 trillion in total investments since President Trump returned to
  office include:
  - General Motors is investing over $6 billion in manufacturing across
    the United States, $4 billion of which will be at its Orion Assembly
    plant in Michigan. Brussels-based biopharmaceutical company UCB is
    investing $2 billion in a manufacturing facility in Georgia.

  <!-- -->

  - Japan is investing $21 billion to build two chip factories in New
    York that will be jointly owned by the United States and Japan and
    operated by GlobalFoundries.  
- In September 2026, for the ninth consecutive month, economic activity
  in manufacturing continued to expand under President Trump’s
  leadership, and in July 2026, U.S. manufacturing activity expanded at
  its fastest pace in over four years.
- While President Trump continues to lock in fair and reciprocal trade
  deals with nearly twenty trading partners across the globe, Canada
  continues to retaliate against the United States for its efforts to
  correct trade imbalances and protect American workers, farmers, and
  businesses.
- Over the past year and a half, only two countries have chosen to
  retaliate against President Trump’s tariffs rather than negotiate a
  deal with the United States: The People’s Republic of China and
  Canada.
- Today’s action reflects President Trump’s continued efforts to defend
  American workers and ensure fair trade, offsetting the burden and
  disadvantage on U.S. commerce from Canada’s discriminatory treatment.
