title: Presidential Message on General Pulaski Memorial Day
---
 
Today, we honor the heroic life and legacy of Brigadier General Casimir
Pulaski—a son of Poland turned American warrior, a father of
Independence, and a martyr of Liberty, whose courage breathed life into
the Continental Army, advancing the sacred cause of freedom during the
Revolutionary War.

Born in Warsaw, Poland, in 1745, Casimir Pulaski proved himself to be a
battle-hardened patriot from an early age, courageously fighting to
defend the freedom of his countrymen against Russian aggression. 
Recognizing his extraordinary military skill, fearless spirit, and
profound devotion to liberty, Benjamin Franklin recruited him to cross
the Atlantic Ocean and join the Continental Army’s righteous fight for
Independence.

Without hesitation, Pulaski arrived on our shores in the summer of 1777
and, within weeks, displayed his bravery and tactical expertise at the
Battle of Brandywine against the might of the British Empire.  When
American lines collapsed under a devastating British flanking attack,
threatening the fate of our young Republic, Pulaski rose to the call of
history and gathered a small band of horsemen to lead a defiant charge
against the British, allowing George Washington and his men to escape
death and retreat to safety. 

In the year following, Pulaski transformed a ragtag cavalry into a
unified, disciplined fighting force capable of defeating the mightiest
army in the world, earning him the title “Father of the American
Cavalry.”  In October 1779, he gave his life for the cause of liberty
while leading a cavalry charge during the Siege of Savannah—etching his
name forever into the heart of America.  

Pulaski’s legacy of sacrifice, courage, and strength is a testament to
the unyielding fortitude of the Polish people.  From overcoming the
brutal despotism of Nazi Germany to defeating the devastating tyranny of
communism, they have shown the world the unstoppable power of freedom
and the unbreakable resolve of the human spirit, so magnificently
embodied in the life and legacy of General Casimir Pulaski.  As I
proudly declared in Warsaw during my first term, the United States is
immeasurably enriched by the contributions of Polish Americans, who have
defended our freedom and made our Nation greater, stronger, and more
prosperous.  Bound by our shared love of liberty, the United States and
Poland enjoy a steadfast bond and friendship that my Administration is
continuing to strengthen.  

As we pay tribute to a remarkable warrior who gave his life for our
young Republic, we remember General Pulaski for his battlefield
brilliance and the unwavering devotion to freedom that burned within him
until his very last breath.  Inspired by his bravery and patriotism, may
we continue to cherish, defend, and carry forward the sacred flame of
liberty that he and generations of Americans shed their blood to
preserve.
