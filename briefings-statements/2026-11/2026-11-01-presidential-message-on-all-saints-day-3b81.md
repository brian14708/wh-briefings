title: "Presidential Message on All Saints\u2019 Day"
---
 
On All Saints’ Day, I join Christians across our Nation in honoring the
men and women through the centuries whose extraordinary lives of
faithfulness reveal the great goodness and love to which human beings
can aspire.

Every generation of Americans has understood the profound power of the
example of a heroic and holy life, which is why the names of saints are
so interwoven throughout the American landscape.  We have San Francisco,
California, named for Saint Francis of Assisi; San Antonio, Texas, named
for Saint Anthony of Padua; St. Louis, Missouri, named for a saintly
king of France; and St. Paul, Minnesota, named for the great Apostle.
 From Santa Monica, California, to St. Augustine, Florida, saints greet
us on maps and road signs across the country.

Saints are just as present in the institutions we know and trust.
 Families turn to St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital when a child is
gravely ill.  Young Americans study at Saint John’s University, Saint
Louis University, Saint Bonaventure University, and many other colleges
and universities named for saints.  Every March, millions of Americans
celebrate Saint Patrick’s Day with parades, music, and the wearing of
green.

Even our professional sports carry this legacy.  When New Orleans
received its National Football League franchise on November 1, 1966, the
date was no accident:  It was All Saints’ Day.  A local businessman
championed the name “Saints,” inspired by the city’s celebrated jazz
anthem, “When the Saints Go Marching In.”

From sea to shining sea, these names are a constant reminder that a
great civilization needs more than material success.  It needs examples
of goodness.  It needs heroes not only of strength and achievement but
also of mercy, sacrifice, humility, and love.

In the United States, we especially honor Saint Junípero Serra, who
carried the Christian faith through the missions of California; Saint
Elizabeth Ann Seton, who helped build Catholic education in America;
Saint Katharine Drexel, who devoted her fortune and her life to
educating and serving poor and impoverished communities; and Saint
Frances Xavier Cabrini, who established schools, orphanages, and
hospitals, putting Christian charity into action and bringing the hope
of Jesus to those most in need.  Countless other saints—parents,
teachers, nurses, workers, missionaries, and neighbors whose names
history never recorded—quietly showed what holiness can look like in
ordinary life.

All Saints’ Day proclaims a truth at the heart of the Christian faith: 
Death does not have the final word.  Christians believe that through the
death and Resurrection of Jesus Christ, the grave has been conquered and
eternal life has been opened to all who believe in Him.  The saints are
witnesses to that promise.  We honor them not merely as heroes of the
past but also as signs of the hope that extends to every human life and
reaches beyond our earthly existence.

On this All Saints’ Day, Melania and I offer our warmest greetings to
all who observe this holy feast.  I will always defend the freedom of
Americans to express their faith openly and without fear.  May the lives
of the saints renew our gratitude for our Nation’s spiritual
inheritance; strengthen our resolve to love and serve one another; and
inspire us to live with the faith, courage, and charity that make a
people—and a nation—forever great.
