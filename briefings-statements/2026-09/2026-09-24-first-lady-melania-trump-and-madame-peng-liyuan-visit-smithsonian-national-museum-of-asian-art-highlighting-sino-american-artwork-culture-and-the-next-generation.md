title: First Lady Melania Trump and Madame Peng Liyuan Visit Smithsonian National
  Museum of Asian Art Highlighting Sino-American Artwork, Culture, and the Next Generation
---
 
First Lady Melania Trump and Madame Peng Liyuan visited the Smithsonian
National Museum of Asian Art in Washington, DC today, where they
explored works spanning centuries of artistic achievement and cultural
exchange and shared a special moment with students from the Washington,
DC area.

Upon arrival, the First Lady and Madame Peng were welcomed by leadership
of the Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art before beginning their
tour of the museum’s collections.

The First Lady and Madame Peng then viewed the Smithsonian’s celebrated
[Peacock
Room](https://us.list-manage.com/12Gvi1QfSfZ?e=e7ee628798&c2id=5088a0791de997a1c2fbc9e9c47ffa6f),
one of the museum’s most distinctive and immersive works of art.

Created by American artist James McNeill Whistler in 1876–77, the
Peacock Room transforms an entire interior into a dazzling composition
of blue, green, and gold. Inspired in significant part by Chinese
porcelain and Japanese design, Whistler covered the room with shimmering
patterns and fanciful depictions of peacocks, creating a singular work
in which painting, architecture, and decorative arts converge. Purchased
by American collector Charles Lang Freer in 1904, the room was
transported from London to the United States and has been a centerpiece
of the museum in Washington since 1923.

Today, the Peacock Room stands as a remarkable meeting point of American
art and Asian artistic traditions, reflecting the ways artists and
collectors have found inspiration across cultures and generations.

The First Lady and Madame Peng continued their visit with a viewing of a
Qianlong-period Chinese porcelain
[vase](https://us.list-manage.com/GgDjWsbpeMB?e=e7ee628798&c2id=5088a0791de997a1c2fbc9e9c47ffa6f),
dating to the 18th century. Produced in Jingdezhen during the Qing
dynasty, the vase features elaborate floral decoration executed in
colorful famille rose enamels, gold, and iron red over a pale glaze. The
work is part of the Freer Gallery of Art’s collection and reflects the
extraordinary technical and decorative sophistication of imperial
Chinese porcelain during the Qianlong era.

Museum leadership who served as guides for the visit were Jan Stuart,
Melvin R. Seiden Curator of Chinese Art at the National Museum of Asian
Art, and Chase Robinson, Dame Jillian Sackler Director of the Arthur M.
Sackler Gallery and Freer Gallery of Art.

The visit concluded with a musical performance by the youth choir from
Hope Chinese School, who performed “Jasmine Flower” and “Songs & Smiles”
for the First Lady and Madame Peng. The young students’ performance
offered a joyful and memorable conclusion to the visit, bringing
together the beauty of artistic expression, the appreciation of cultural
heritage, and the shared experience of young people discovering the
enduring power of art and music.
