title: Presidential Message on National Recovery Month
---
 
During National Recovery Month, we celebrate the more than 23 million
Americans who have found a pathway from addiction to recovery, we
recognize those who have helped them reclaim their lives, and we renew
our commitment to supporting those still struggling to find hope and
healing. 

Throughout every community in America, the effects of drug and alcohol
addiction are felt in the lives of families, friends, and neighbors. 
Today, 48.4 million Americans suffer from addiction, and 69,973
Americans died last year to overdose.  Every American deserves to live
free from the grip of addiction—and my Administration is wholly
committed to making that possible by preventing drug use before it
starts, providing treatment at the earliest opportunity, and helping
more Americans lead engaged and productive lives in recovery.  

In January, I was proud to establish the Great American Recovery
Initiative, coordinating a national response to the disease of addiction
across government, healthcare, faith communities, and the private sector
while fostering a culture that celebrates recovery.  I also signed an
Executive Order on Ending Crime and Disorder on America’s Streets to
make our communities safer and help homeless Americans access addiction
and mental health services needed to rebuild their lives.  I am
directing the Food and Drug Administration to prioritize non-opioid
options for chronic pain, and I signed the SUPPORT for Patients and
Communities Reauthorization Act to strengthen federal grant programs for
treatment, overdose response, and mental health care.  We are also
stopping deadly drugs before they ever reach our shores.  I signed the
HALT Fentanyl Act into law, classifying all fentanyl-related substances
as Schedule I drugs; formally designated major international drug
cartels as foreign terrorist organizations; sealed our border; and
empowered our brave Border Patrol and Armed Forces with the resources
needed to carry out massive drug seizures to keep fentanyl off our
streets. 

Recovery is a testament to the strength of the American Spirit and the
promise of a second chance.  This month, we stand with those on the road
to recovery and the families and communities who support them along the
way.  Together, we will help more Americans break free from addiction
and lead healthy, productive, and fulfilling lives.
