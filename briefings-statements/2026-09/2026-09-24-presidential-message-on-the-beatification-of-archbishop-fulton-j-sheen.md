title: Presidential Message on the Beatification of Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen
---
 
Melania and I join all those celebrating the life and legacy of
Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen—the Catholic priest who pioneered the use of
media to speak the truth, preach the Gospel, and bring many souls closer
to Christ.  Today, Archbishop Sheen will become the first-ever
American-born bishop to be beatified by the Catholic Church.

Born on the plains of central Illinois at the twilight of the nineteenth
century, Fulton Sheen was blessed with extraordinary intelligence, good
humor, and eloquence.  After his ordination to the priesthood, he spent
more than two decades teaching, lecturing, and broadcasting on the radio
before he was consecrated Auxiliary Bishop of New York in 1951.  With a
growing reputation as a world-class preacher and evangelist, he then
launched his renowned national television series, *Life Is Worth
Living*, which soon captivated the entire country.

On his show, Archbishop Sheen lectured before a live audience on an
array of theological, philosophical, and political topics.  Dressed in
striking priestly robes and armed with only a chalk stick and
blackboard, he quickly emerged as a primetime sensation—boldly bringing
the teachings of Christianity to a world threatened by the titanic
forces of communism.

Archbishop Sheen shocked the entertainment industry by besting American
legends like Milton Berle and Frank Sinatra in weekly ratings, winning
an Emmy award for Most Outstanding Television Personality, and appearing
on the cover of *Time* magazine.  For years, his sharp wit and piercing
intellect filled millions of American living rooms and converted
thousands—including national politicians, Hollywood celebrities, and
communist leaders—to the Catholic faith.  His program attracted an
estimated 30 million weekly viewers, ultimately becoming one of the most
watched religious programs in the history of television.  Through it
all, Archbishop Sheen used his platform not for his own sake but to
strengthen our Nation with the good news of the Gospel.

In the 1960s, against the rising tide of anti-God extremism in American
culture, Archbishop Sheen turned to the camera to identify a “new type
of violence that’s sweeping our country, our schools, and our streets.” 
This violence, he warned in his later career *The Fulton Sheen Program*,
“involves destruction of everything that is in the past.”  He continued,
“Our Nation is too full of those that are crying, “Down… Down with
schools.  Down with the churches.  Down with teachers.  Down with
government.  Down with the police.  Can you build anything down?” he
implored the Nation.  “You cannot… Let’s begin now to use the word
‘Up.’  Up from all of this filth.  Up from this violence… Up—up to God.”

Sheen was later appointed as the Bishop of Rochester, New York, and the
titular Archbishop of Newport, Wales.  In October 1979, Pope John Paul
II, the leader of the Roman Catholic Church, embraced Archbishop Sheen
in Saint Patrick’s Cathedral in New York City, telling him, “You have
written and spoken well of the Lord Jesus.  You are a loyal son of the
Church.”  Archbishop Sheen died just two months later while praying in
his private chapel.

Archbishop Fulton Sheen’s legacy endures in homes, churches, and
communities all across our country.  Countless Americans still watch
recordings of his broadcasts and read his books.  The Archbishop Fulton
J. Sheen Museum in his native Illinois attracts thousands of visitors
every year.  And today, as thousands of Americans gather in St. Louis,
Missouri, for his Beatification Mass, we pray that Archbishop Sheen’s
heroic commitment to nourishing the soul of our country will inspire
others to follow his example and lift our Nation upward and ever higher
to God.
