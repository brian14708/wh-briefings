title: Presidential Message on the Anniversary of the Battle of Fort McHenry
---
 
In September of 1814, 1,000 American patriots heroically defended our
young Nation’s sovereignty at the Battle of Fort McHenry, where they
halted the British invasion of Baltimore in a critical turning point in
the War of 1812.  Despite relentless cannon bombardment from the most
powerful navy of its age, American forces stood strong.  When dawn broke
the next morning, the majestic Stars and Stripes remained proudly flying
in full glory over the war-torn Fort McHenry.

The battle came at a perilous moment in our young Nation.  British
troops had stormed through the United States, setting fire to the White
House and United States Capitol in a campaign to crush American
willpower and restore British dominion over our hemisphere.  After their
attack on Washington, the British turned their sights north toward
Baltimore with the aim of capturing one of America’s most vital seaports
and forcing a surrender on their terms.  With the future of our young
Nation at stake, the citizens of Maryland rallied to arms, forming a
militia and preparing their city for battle.  Standing watch at Fort
McHenry, these courageous soldiers and volunteers steeled themselves to
defend the stronghold that guarded Baltimore Harbor.

On the morning of September 13, the British began their brutal naval
assault on Fort McHenry, firing a barrage of exploding rockets and
cannonballs.  For 27 long, grueling hours, the American soldiers at Fort
McHenry endured withering fire from the British fleet, but their
unflinching resolve and masterful defenses saved the fort from
destruction.  Exhausted, frustrated, and dismayed, British troops
abandoned their failed assault the next morning, sailing away in
retreat.

Watching the battle unfold from a nearby ship, American lawyer Francis
Scott Key feared the fort had succumbed to the British bombardment
overnight.  But when the dawn’s early light gleamed on the fort the next
morning, Key’s anxious eyes beheld Old Glory gallantly streaming high in
defiance and proudly signaling an American victory.  Struck with a
patriotic zeal, he immortalized America’s triumph that day by writing
the most legendary ode to freedom ever put to paper:  “The Star-Spangled
Banner,” now enshrined as our National Anthem.

Two hundred and twelve years after this seminal battle, we honor the
unwavering determination and persistence of the American forces that
fought to preserve our Nation’s freedom at Fort McHenry.  Their
sacrifice and courage remind us that our freedom only endures through
the tenacity and resolution of the patriots willing to defend it, both
at home and abroad. 

As we continue the work of saving our country, defending our values,
reclaiming our sovereignty, and restoring our national pride—and as we
reflect on 250 years of glorious American Independence—may the
Star-Spangled Banner ever wave over the land of the free and the home of
the brave.
