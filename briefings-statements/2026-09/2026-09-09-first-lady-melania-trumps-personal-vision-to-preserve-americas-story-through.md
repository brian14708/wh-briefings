First Lady Melania Trump shared her forward-looking vision on the
generational significance of institutional design, declaring that, “The
White House stands as an architectural autobiography of America,
embodying the enduring values of ambition, democracy, and civic life
through its design, craftmanship, and the objects within.”

First Lady Melania Trump’s elevated creative vision has played a
significant and meaningful role, shaping architecture, landscape,
preserving historic interiors, and enhancing decorative arts to carry
the White House forward.

First Lady Melania Trump’s most substantial design accomplishments
include the award-winning design and construction of the White House
Tennis Pavilion, the complete renovation of the Queens’ Bedroom and the
Queens’ Bathroom, the conservation of the historic Zuber wall covering
in the President’s Dining Room; and the 2020 restoration of the Rose
Garden, which renewed its historic design while revamping infrastructure
and accessibility. The First Lady affirmed that important design
“fosters collaboration, conveys dignity, inspires ambition, and creates
a sense of belonging to something larger.”

First Lady Melania Trump’s White House preservation work also
encompassed the East Room floor, State Entrance and Hall marble floors,
President’s Elevator, White House Bowling Alley, and Children’s Garden,
all demonstrating a broader devotion to American craftsmanship and the
preservation of the People’s House.  Mrs. Trump highlighted, “Within the
walls of the White House, these experiences become essential to our
national identity.”

Notably, her design work has extended beyond the White House grounds to
Camp David, further broadening her approach to preserving and evolving
America’s historic presidential spaces. The First Lady exclaimed, “Great
design transcends aesthetic appeal; it shapes our lived experiences.”

The First Lady’s comments were presented from the White House Rose
Garden to honor the 2026 White House Historical Association Presidential
Sites Summit. This convening brings together more than 200 leaders from
presidential sites nationwide, reflecting on the America 250
celebration, with a view to the future.

The full remarks by First Lady Melania Trump are below.

**The White House: America’s Architectural Autobiography**

The White House stands as an architectural autobiography of America,
embodying the enduring values of ambition, democracy, and civic life
through its design, craftsmanship, and the objects within.

Generation after generation, America’s ideals and stories are woven
directly into the fabric of the White House.

Design is a meticulous process that involves a multitude of choices—each
line, texture, and play of natural light contributes to an overarching
narrative. No detail is too small; what may seem insignificant today can
influence how individuals connect with one another and perceive their
place in history.

Within the walls of the White House, these experiences become essential
to our national identity.

I encountered this truth firsthand when I sought to uncover the
exquisite American War of Independence wallpaper designed by French
artist Jean-Julien Deltil and created by the firm Jean Zuber and Co.
This wallpaper was hidden behind the walls of the President’s Dining
Room in the private Family Quarters. Created in 1852 and initially
acquired by the Astor family, this exceptional piece was ultimately
concealed beneath a wall treatment. My mission to restore and reveal it
was driven by a commitment to preserving a vital piece of our American
narrative. The successful unveiling of this work was not merely an act
of restoration; it was an investment in the future of our nation’s
story.

Great design transcends aesthetic appeal; it shapes our lived
experiences.

It fosters collaboration, conveys dignity, inspires ambition, and
creates a sense of belonging to something larger. This philosophy guided
my work on the White House Tennis Pavilion, designed in collaboration
with the Trust for the National Mall, the Executive Residence, and the
National Park Service, and approved by the Committee for the
Preservation of the White House, the Commission of Fine Arts, and the
National Capital Planning Commission in 2018. Every detail was
thoughtfully considered to ensure the new structure harmonized with the
historic neoclassical character of the White House Grounds.

Completed in 2020, the White House Tennis Pavilion later earned two
distinguished architectural awards: the 2025 Palladio Award and the John
Russell Pope Award. These honors recognize its design inspiration, which
echoes the White House itself through doric columns, fanlight windows,
limestone cladding, and a copper roof.

The Pavilion exemplifies the power of thoughtful design and serves as a
bridge that connects people and generations through shared history.

As stewards of the places shaped by those before us, we are tasked with
making decisions that honor future generations. It is our duty to ensure
that the choices we make today are worthy of the legacy we pass forward.
And now, it is my honor to introduce John Rogers, the Chairman of the
Board of the White House Historical Association. John is not just a
remarkable leader; he is a steadfast ally and supporter, dedicated to
preserving the rich heritage of our nation’s most iconic residence. His
passion for history and commitment to education inspire all of us to
champion the values and stories that shape our democracy. With John’s
unwavering support, we are empowered to continue our vital mission and
ensure that the legacy of the White House endures for generations to
come.
