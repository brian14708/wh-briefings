title: Presidential Message on National Park Week
---
 
America’s vast and wondrous national parks stand at the heart of our
story, preserving the lands our ancestors cherished and the unmatched
splendor of a continent like no other.  During National Park Week, we
celebrate the rich cultural heritage, grandeur, and exceptionalism found
in the more than 400 national parks and monuments across our land.

On August 25, 1916, the National Park Service was established to protect
our Nation’s natural resources, historic landmarks, and magnificent
landscapes.  For more than a century, our treasured parks, battlefields,
and monuments have stood as hallmarks of the American Spirit, drawing
enthusiasts from all walks of life to seek out places of historical
significance along with some of the world’s most incredible natural
wonders—from coastal landscapes, pristine glacial lakes, and rugged
forests to rushing waterfalls, hiking trails, and wild game found
nowhere else on Earth.

My Administration is committed to protecting every acre of our lands and
preserving the cleanest air and water in the world.  During my first
term, I was proud to sign the Great American Outdoors Act—the most
significant conservation policy since President Teddy Roosevelt—to
address prior mismanagement, maintenance backlogs, and critical
infrastructure needs in our national parks and public lands.  Upon
returning to office, I signed the Make America Beautiful Again Executive
Order, establishing a commission devoted to promoting voluntary
stewardship of our lands, expanding outdoor access, cutting needless
regulations, and recovering imperiled species all while growing our
economy and protecting the time-honored integrity of our national parks.
 In response, the Commission issued its Make America Beautiful Again
report, calling on the American people, private businesses, and
non-profit groups to partner with the federal government and each other
to ensure that the next 250 years of our great nation are beautiful,
successful, and secure.  To mark this milestone year for the National
Park Service, I am pleased to announce that on August 25 our national
parks will be open free of charge to residents of the United States,
giving families the opportunity to see firsthand the natural beauty of
our magnificent Republic.  Under my leadership, we will make America’s
Great Outdoors a source of national pride and breathtaking splendor once
again, vowing always to defend our God-given heritage through
responsible conservation and bold restoration efforts. 

As our Nation commemorates 250 glorious years of Independence, we give
thanks for the abundant natural resources of our homeland and for the
generations of Americans whose labor preserved these treasures. 
Together, we will ensure that our majestic national parks endure as a
source of adventure, respite, inspiration, and awe for generations to
come.
