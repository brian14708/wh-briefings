Executive Summary

The Council of Economic Advisers (CEA) has estimated the economic
benefits of permitting reform. The National Environmental Policy Act
(NEPA) acts as an umbrella for environmental review, requiring agencies
to evaluate environmental impacts before projects can proceed. Impacted
projects include both direct federal actions and private projects
requiring federal permits. An environmental impact statement (EIS)
typically catalogs the other federal permits that must be obtained –
under laws such as the Clean Water Act, Endangered Species Act, and
National Historic Preservation Act – for the project to proceed. EISs
published in recent years have averaged roughly 1,700 pages including
appendixes, and agencies took on average 4.2 years to complete them.
 Long implementation delays discourage investment because they force
investors to wait longer for returns to be realized, resulting in
insufficient infrastructure investment and a lower quantity of capital
per worker, which leads to higher prices and lower output.

We develop a model to analyze reforms which limit how long projects may
be delayed.  The Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA)
would institute enforceable NEPA review deadlines.  In particular,
agencies would be required to complete EISs within two years. **We find
that reducing review times down to the levels required by the BAAJA
would:**

- Increase long-run GDP by between 0.28 percent and 0.56 percent (i.e.,
  $82 billion to $164 billion in additional output each year).
- Increase annual tax revenue by $14 billion to $28 billion.
- Increase real wages by $306 to $637 per year for the average
  household.
- Reduce electric bills by $178 to $251 per year for the average
  household.
- Reduce other expenses by $125 to $176 per year for the average
  household.

**Reducing completion time even more aggressively – all the way down to
1 year – would:**

- Increase long-run GDP by between 0.41 percent and 0.83 percent (i.e.,
  $120 billion to $243 billion in additional output per year).
- Increase annual tax revenue by $20 to $41 billion.
- Increase real wages by $445 to $922 per year for the average
  household.
- Reduce electric bills by $259 to $366 per year for the average
  household.
- Reduce other expenses by $184 to $257 per year for the average
  household.

Our findings are likely conservative for two reasons. First, we limit
our analysis to Environmental Impact Statements (EIS) and do not account
for Environmental Assessments (EA). While EAs are less onerous, they are
significantly more numerous and similarly add to delays in realized
investment. Second, NEPA allows for litigation delays, which introduces
additional uncertainty to the investment decision. In standard models of
investment, additional uncertainty leads to lower investment. Our
analysis does not quantify the effects of this potential reduction in
uncertainty nor the reduction in the financial costs of litigation.
